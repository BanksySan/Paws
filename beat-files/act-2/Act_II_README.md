# Act II — README

## Purpose

Act II removes Cat's and Dog's competence by placing each inside the other's body.

The body swap should not immediately create empathy.

At first, it should make both animals more certain that the other's body is badly designed.

Each tries to solve problems using the strategies that have always worked before.

Those strategies now fail.

The governing progression is:

**wrong body → old strategy → failure → frustration → first crack in certainty**

---

## Starting state

At the beginning of Act II, Cat and Dog have exchanged bodies.

Neither understands how or why.

Each still thinks like themselves.

### Cat inside Dog's body

Cat initially tries to use:

- precision;
- route planning;
- distant observation;
- stillness;
- small gaps;
- vertical movement.

Dog's body does not respond the way she expects.

### Dog inside Cat's body

Dog initially tries to use:

- direct approach;
- strength;
- pushing;
- proximity;
- social engagement;
- momentum.

Cat's body cannot support those strategies in the same way.

---

## Core dramatic principle

The body swap should first produce:

> This body is terrible.

Only later should that become:

> I have been using it wrongly.

Act II is mainly concerned with reaching the point where that second thought becomes possible.

It does not need to produce full understanding yet.

---

## Core jobs of the act

Act II should establish:

- Cat's loss of her familiar physical precision.
- Dog's loss of strength and physical security.
- The dramatic difference in scale experienced from Cat's body.
- Dog's body as heavy, strong and momentum-driven.
- Cat's body as small, light and physically vulnerable to large moving objects.
- Cat's first encounter with Dog's overwhelming smell-world.
- Dog's first encounter with Cat's unusually detailed hearing and low-light/spatial perception.
- Familiar household routines becoming strange because the humans treat each body according to its usual identity.
- Cat discovering that Dog's proximity to humans is partly sensory and social, not simply habit.
- Dog discovering that being approached by something large can feel dangerous even when he knows intellectually that it is friendly.
- The first meaningful crack in each animal's assumptions.

---

## Suggested chapter function

### Cat — Something is terribly wrong

Cat discovers that Dog's body has:

- weight;
- momentum;
- large feet;
- reduced access to familiar routes;
- an overwhelming amount of scent information.

She initially experiences these as defects.

### Dog — The world has become enormous

Dog discovers:

- smallness;
- lack of pushing strength;
- the importance of human feet, doors and large moving bodies;
- unexpectedly precise hearing.

He initially experiences Cat's body as inadequate.

### Cat — Humans complicate everything

The humans treat Cat-in-Dog as Dog.

This exposes Dog's household routines and the sensory/social importance of the family.

### Dog — Outside is different when you are small

Dog-in-Cat encounters a large but harmless or familiar animal.

His mind may know that the approach is safe.

His new body's scale and vulnerability tell him something else.

This should provide the strongest Act II crack in certainty.

---

## Sensory progression

### Cat in Dog

Smell should initially be:

**too much**

before becoming:

**useful structure**

in Act III.

### Dog in Cat

Hearing, low-light awareness and spatial sensitivity should initially be:

**too much / confusing / restrictive**

before becoming:

**useful information**

in Act III.

---

## Physical progression

### Cat's mistake

Cat should initially compare Dog's body against the things her own body does well.

That makes Dog's body seem:

- clumsy;
- noisy;
- overlarge;
- poorly suited to narrow or elevated routes.

She has not yet discovered what strength, momentum and robustness make possible.

### Dog's mistake

Dog should initially compare Cat's body against the things his own body does well.

That makes Cat's body seem:

- weak;
- too small;
- unable to push;
- physically insecure.

He has not yet discovered what agility, height, gaps, concealment and precision make possible.

---

## Emotional requirement

The act should contain frustration.

The swap must cost them something.

They should miss their own:

- senses;
- movement;
- reach;
- habits;
- ordinary competence.

Understanding will matter more if it is earned through failure rather than granted by the magical event itself.

---

## End state

By the end of Act II:

- neither animal fully understands the other;
- neither has mastered the new body;
- both have experienced at least one moment that contradicts an old assumption;
- the other's behaviour has become harder to dismiss as merely foolish.

The key transition is:

> This body is terrible.

becoming, for the first time:

> Maybe there is something I don't understand.

---

## Guardrails

### Do not make the swap instantly intuitive

The wrong body should feel genuinely wrong.

### Do not make either animal magically gain the other's instincts

They possess the body, not the accumulated understanding of how to use it.

### Do not turn the act into a zoology lesson

Discoveries should arise through attempts, failures, sensations and consequences.

### Do not make vulnerability equal weakness

Dog-in-Cat should initially feel weak because he compares Cat's body to Dog's.

The story should later reveal that Cat experiences the same body as extremely capable.

### Do not resolve their relationship too soon

Act II is disorientation, not reconciliation.

---

## Author questions to keep asking

- What familiar strategy is the POV character trying to use?
- Why did that strategy work in their original body?
- Why does it fail now?
- What information is the new body providing that the character does not yet know how to interpret?
- What physical capability have they lost?
- What capability is present but still undiscovered?
- What old judgement becomes slightly harder to maintain?

---

## Act II north star

**The swap does not initially teach Cat and Dog that the other was right.**

**It teaches them that their own rules are no longer enough.**
