# Act I — Beat File

## Purpose of the Act

Act I should establish:

- Dog as big, physical, sociable, impulsive, and smell-led.
- Cat as small, precise, cautious, self-directed, and sound/sight-led.
- Their different relationships with the humans.
- Their different attitudes to proximity, weather, danger, and movement.
- The fact that neither really understands the other's behaviour.
- The household routine, so that the later disruption has something normal to disrupt.
- The physical and emotional rules that will become important after the body swap.

Most importantly:

> **Dog assumes everyone wants company. Cat assumes everyone should control their own space.**

Neither is wrong.

---

## Sequence 1 — Morning Begins

### Beat 1 — Someone wakes up

The house is still mostly quiet.

Dog notices a human is awake.

To Dog:

**A person going somewhere without Dog makes no sense.**

The human starts downstairs.

Dog follows.

There is no decision involved.

Going downstairs is happening, therefore Dog is going downstairs.

This can also establish the bathroom-door joke very early if we want it:

A parent goes into the bathroom.

The door closes.

Dog stops outside.

Confused.

Why has the journey continued without him?

He waits outside until the human emerges.

Everything is restored.

#### What this establishes

- Dog's social instinct.
- Dog follows rather than surveys.
- Closed doors are incomprehensible to him.
- His basic expectation is **togetherness**.

---

## Sequence 2 — Dog and the Snow

### Beat 2 — The back door opens

Morning routine.

Dog needs to go outside to do his business.

The parent opens the back door.

There is snow.

Dog is delighted.

He doesn't inspect it first.

He doesn't consider the temperature.

He launches himself straight outside.

### Beat 3 — Dog in his element

Dog bounds through the snow.

Possible details:

- Nose straight down into it.
- Snuffling around where something passed overnight.
- Investigating smells beneath the snow.
- Jumping through deeper patches.
- Kicking snow behind him.
- Perhaps rolling.
- Momentarily forgetting why he was sent out.

The main point is sheer physical enthusiasm.

Dog's world here is:

**movement + scent + excitement.**

Snow is not an obstacle.

Snow is additional information.

Eventually he remembers his actual purpose and does what he came out to do.

Then back to the house.

---

## Sequence 3 — The Towel Escape

### Beat 4 — Dog comes back in

Dog is wet, snowy, and extremely pleased with himself.

The parent has a towel waiting.

Dog likes the towel.

The rubbing is excellent.

Neck. Shoulders. Back.

Basically scratching.

But there's a problem:

**Being dried requires remaining in roughly one place.**

Dog is not enthusiastic about this part.

He wriggles.

Turns around.

Leans into the rubbing.

Moves away.

Comes back.

The parent is trying to dry an animal who simultaneously enjoys the process and refuses to cooperate with it.

### Beat 5 — Dog escapes

Something catches his attention upstairs.

Perhaps a child moving.

Perhaps Cat.

Perhaps simply the realisation that people are upstairs and Dog currently isn't.

Dog bolts.

Parent:

> “Dog!”

Wet pawprints.

Another shout:

> “Don't let him upstairs!”

Too late.

Someone calls:

> “Has he been dried?”

Answer:

> “No!”

Now Dog is thundering up the stairs.

#### What this establishes

Dog isn't deliberately disobedient.

His problem is **immediacy**.

Whatever is happening now is enormously important.

Whatever was happening three seconds ago has already lost the competition.

---

## Sequence 4 — Cat on the Bed

### Beat 6 — Cat has been comfortable all morning

Cut to Cat.

She is on a bed.

Warm.

Quiet.

Possibly curled into the indentation left by one of the humans.

Cat has been aware of almost everything happening downstairs.

She heard:

- The back door.
- Dog crashing through the snow.
- Dog returning.
- Humans shouting.
- Dog escaping.
- Dog beginning to climb the stairs.

She doesn't need to investigate.

She already knows what is happening.

This is an early demonstration of the difference between them.

Dog must **go to things**.

Cat can often understand things **from where she is**.

### Beat 7 — Incoming Dog

Thump.

Thump.

Thump.

Dog approaches.

Cat's tail begins to move.

Not happiness.

Warning.

Dog arrives.

Sees Cat.

Excellent.

Cat is here!

Dog's tail begins wagging too.

Same outward behaviour.

Completely different meaning.

### Beat 8 — Dog jumps onto the bed

Dog doesn't creep up.

He doesn't check whether Cat wants company.

He jumps straight onto the bed beside her.

**WHUMP.**

The mattress drops under his weight.

Dog is:

- Large.
- Cold.
- Wet.
- Noisy.
- Excited.

Cat is:

- Small.
- Warm.
- Dry.
- Resting.

Cat flees.

Probably up onto something higher.

Hiss.

Dog stops.

Baffled.

He came to say hello.

Cat had been moving her tail.

He thought she was pleased.

Why is she angry?

### Beat 9 — The humans arrive

Parent appears.

Sees:

- Damp bedding.
- Wet Dog.
- Cat glaring from safety.

> “Dog!”

Dog looks around.

What?

From Dog's perspective, the morning has so far consisted of:

**Snow! Towel! Running! Cat!**

Everything has been excellent.

Why is everyone cross?

#### What this establishes

This is probably the clearest miniature of their whole relationship.

**Dog:** I was being friendly.

**Cat:** I told you not to come closer.

---

## Sequence 5 — The Family at Breakfast

### Beat 10 — Everyone gathers

The household moves towards breakfast.

This gives us time to meet the parents and children naturally.

Dog accompanies whoever is moving.

Someone walks into the kitchen: Dog follows.

Someone goes back upstairs for something: Dog considers following.

Someone sits down: Dog settles nearby.

Then another person gets up.

New decision.

Cat arrives differently.

She appears when she chooses to.

Possibly nobody sees exactly where she came from.

### Beat 11 — Human relationships

This is where we can establish that the children treat the animals differently.

Perhaps one child is very physical with Dog:

- Hugs.
- Pats.
- Wrestling.
- Throwing toys.

Dog loves this.

The same child may occasionally try something similar with Cat and get nowhere.

Another child has learnt Cat's rules:

- Waits.
- Offers a hand.
- Strokes her only when invited.
- Lets her leave.

This lets us demonstrate without exposition that each animal has different needs.

### Beat 12 — Dog's breakfast world

Dog is surrounded by smell.

Breakfast isn't just breakfast.

It is:

- Toast.
- Butter.
- Cereal.
- Yesterday's dinner bin.
- Child's socks.
- Wet boots.
- Something Dad walked through outside.
- Cat.
- Interesting crumbs beneath a chair.

Dog's attention is constantly being pulled outward.

### Beat 13 — Cat's breakfast world

Cat's attention is different.

She notices:

- Positions.
- Movement.
- Sound.
- Escape routes.
- Dog approaching.
- Chair legs.
- Windows.
- Where the warm patch is forming.

Same kitchen.

Completely different map.

---

## Sequence 6 — Cat Has to Go Out

### Beat 14 — The door is opened for Cat

At some point a parent remembers Cat needs to go outside too.

Cat is not picked up.

She understands the routine.

The back door opens.

She looks outside.

Snow.

She remains where she is.

Human waits.

Perhaps:

> “Go on.”

Cat looks at the human.

Then back at the snow.

No misunderstanding.

She knows perfectly well what is being requested.

She's just evaluating how unpleasant it is.

### Beat 15 — First paw

Eventually Cat approaches the threshold.

One paw into the snow.

Possibly withdraws it.

Tries again.

She steps carefully.

No bounding.

No rolling.

No enthusiasm.

Dog's tracks are everywhere.

Perhaps Cat uses them.

This is a lovely visual:

Dog smashed enormous holes through the snow.

Cat carefully places her paws into the exposed spaces he's already made.

The same difference again:

**Dog goes through the environment.**

**Cat finds a route through it.**

### Beat 16 — Cat reaches the tree

Slow.

Careful.

Careful.

Then Cat reaches the tree.

Instant transformation.

Up.

Fast.

Precise.

Confident.

Across a branch.

Over the fence.

Gone.

Dog, if watching, cannot understand this.

Why go *up* when you could simply go where you're going?

And why go anywhere alone?

### Beat 17 — Cat disappears

Cat stays away for roughly ten minutes.

Perhaps the humans barely think about it.

Dog does.

Cat has left.

Without Dog.

This is inherently strange.

He may look at the door.

Look at the window.

Sniff around.

Where has she gone?

Why?

### Beat 18 — Cat returns

Nobody necessarily sees Cat arrive.

She is simply suddenly sitting at the back door.

She has done what she needed to do.

She wants breakfast.

Now.

Cat comes inside with minimal snow on her.

Perhaps she delicately shakes one paw.

Dog is still damp.

He looks at her.

Cat looks at him.

Cat cannot understand why anyone would voluntarily roll in frozen water.

Dog cannot understand why anyone would experience snow without enjoying it.

---

## Sequence 7 — Play

This gives us the children and lets us establish movement strategies.

### Beat 19 — Dog's game

Ball.

Throw.

Dog moves immediately.

Object moving = chase.

Dog doesn't pause.

Doesn't strategise.

Doesn't stalk.

He runs.

Probably skids on snow.

Comes back delighted.

Again.

Again.

Again.

One of the children eventually gets bored before Dog does.

This is another useful principle:

**Dog's joy is repetition.**

If something was good once, why wouldn't it be good another twenty-three times?

### Beat 20 — Cat's game

Later, indoors.

A child plays with Cat.

Feather/string/toy mouse.

Cat watches.

Waits.

Toy moves.

Cat does nothing.

Dog may find this baffling.

Then—

Pounce.

Fast, precise, explosive.

The child resets.

Cat waits again.

### Beat 21 — Dog helps

Dog sees the moving toy.

Runs straight at it.

Game over.

Cat leaves.

Dog thinks:

**I helped.**

Cat thinks:

**Idiot.**

Another important contrast:

Dog believes speed means **start now**.

Cat believes speed is something you save until the exact moment you need it.

---

## Sequence 8 — The Quiet Contrast

### Beat 22 — Cat finds warmth

Later, perhaps the winter sun comes through a window.

Cat locates the warm patch.

Settles.

Perfect.

Dog wanders over.

Possibly lies partly in it.

Dog quickly becomes too warm and moves away to a cooler floor.

Cat can't understand this.

Dog can't understand why anyone would deliberately seek extra heat.

This seems small, but it helps establish what their bodies feel like.

### Beat 23 — Together versus alone

We can use this quieter section for the deeper social contrast.

Dog wants to be where the family is.

If several humans are in different rooms, this may become an impossible logistical problem.

He follows one.

Then hears another.

Changes his mind.

Follows them.

He wants the pack assembled.

Cat may deliberately leave the populated room and choose somewhere quiet.

Dog may later find her and be delighted.

Cat had been enjoying not being found.

Neither can understand the other.

---

## Sequence 9 — Small Conflict

### Beat 24 — Dog approaches Cat again

We should probably have one more interaction where the misunderstanding escalates slightly.

Cat is somewhere she considers safe.

Dog approaches.

Cat's tail flicks.

Dog's tail wags.

Cat gives another warning.

Dog keeps coming.

Cat hisses.

Perhaps Dog pauses but then takes one more step.

Cat swipes.

Not a serious attack.

A clear:

**Enough.**

Dog jumps back.

Hurt and confused.

Now we arrive at the emotional positions we want before the swap.

#### Dog's conclusion

Cat doesn't like him.

Possibly Cat is simply mean.

He keeps trying to be friendly.

She keeps reacting badly.

#### Cat's conclusion

Dog refuses to listen.

She gives him every possible warning.

He ignores every one.

Possibly Dog is simply stupid.

And yet—

Neither actually hates the other.

They just don't understand what the other is communicating.

---

## Sequence 10 — Evening Family Life

### Beat 25 — The household settles

Dinner.

Homework.

Television.

Baths.

Bedtime.

Dog moves through all of it with the family.

Perhaps the bathroom-door gag returns.

Child closes bathroom door.

Dog sits outside.

Waits.

Cat walks past.

Cannot fathom why he is sitting there staring at a door.

Dog cannot fathom why Cat isn't concerned that one of their humans has vanished.

### Beat 26 — Affection

This should probably be quite warm.

Dog gets affection physically.

Someone rubs his belly.

Someone hugs him.

He leans against somebody.

Cat gets affection differently.

She climbs onto a lap.

Chooses the position.

Purrs.

Leaves when she's finished.

The humans understand both styles better than the animals understand each other.

This is important because the home should feel loving.

The conflict isn't that the animals live in a bad environment.

It's that they live in a **good home while still misunderstanding each other**.

---

## Sequence 11 — The Final Pre-Swap Clash

### Beat 27 — Same room, different needs

Near bedtime, Cat has found somewhere comfortable.

Dog comes in.

Perhaps again tries to join her.

Cat moves away.

Maybe one of the children says:

> “Why can't you two just get along?”

The humans see the problem.

Neither animal understands the question.

From Dog's perspective:

**I'm trying.**

From Cat's:

**So am I. He won't leave me alone.**

That's a good emotional button.

---

## Sequence 12 — The Swap

We don't necessarily need to decide the exact mechanism yet.

Storm, lightning, strange coincidence, object, or something else.

The important thing is that they go to sleep as themselves.

### Beat 28 — Something happens

They are both in the house.

Something unusual.

Possibly frightening.

Possibly magical.

Possibly barely noticed.

Then sleep.

---

## Sequence 13 — Act I Ending

### Beat 29 — Dog wakes

Something is wrong.

Everything is huge.

The room sounds impossibly loud.

He feels light.

Too light.

Perhaps he attempts to get up and moves far faster than expected.

Then he sees something across the room.

Dog.

His own body.

Standing up.

But looking terrified.

### Beat 30 — Recognition

The two animals stare at one another.

Cat, inside Dog's body, speaks first.

Something like:

> “Dog?”

Dog's immediate thought:

**That was exactly what he had been about to say.**

**End Act I.**

---

# Act I Emotional Progression

The important progression isn't really:

**Cat and Dog fight → Cat and Dog swap bodies.**

It's:

**Cat and Dog repeatedly misinterpret each other's perfectly reasonable behaviour → each becomes increasingly convinced that the other is unreasonable → they are forced into the one situation where misunderstanding becomes impossible.**

The morning sequence — **snow → towel → bed → Cat outside → breakfast** — introduces the household without ever needing a conventional “Here are our characters” scene. Everything is revealed through routine.
