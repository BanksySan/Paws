# Act I — Beat File

## Purpose of the Act

Act I should establish:

- Dog as big, physical, sociable, impulsive, and smell-led.
- Cat as small, precise, cautious, self-directed, and sound/sight-led.
- Their different relationships with the humans.
- Their different attitudes to proximity, weather, danger, and movement.
- The fact that neither really understands the other's behavior.
- The household routine, so that the latest disruption has something normal to disrupt.
- The physical and emotional rules that will become important after the body swap.

Neither is wrong.

## Chapter 1: Cat begins her evening patrol

1. Cat is looking out of the back door. She takes in her world: the garden, the snow, the cold wet slush. She needs to go outside, but she isn't keen on the cold, and she knows there is open ground between her and the safety of the fence.
2. She has her set routes. Out and to the right, quickly across the open ground, then straight onto the fence. From the fence she can see both her garden and the neighbour's garden.
3. The fence gives her options. If the neighbour's garden is clear, she can follow it towards the tree in the far corner. If there is trouble, she can drop back into her own garden and use another route.
4. She sniffs the air and listens carefully, preparing to commit only once she's satisfied that the coast is clear.
5. There's a gentle push from behind her. One of her humans. They do that sometimes. They don't seem to understand that standing in the doorway is part of going outside.
6. Cat has no choice now. She quickly traverses the open ground to the fence. Her claws make the vertical climb easy, and she moves along the narrow top as comfortably as if it were flat ground.
7. The neighbour's garden is quiet, so she follows the fence towards the tree and climbs.
8. She pauses amongst the branches, taking in the view of her own garden and the neighbour's garden before continuing.
9. She follows one of her familiar patrol routes, crossing the boundaries between neighbouring gardens until she reaches the roof of a lean-to, where she stops again.
10. Here she listens.
    1. What has changed since she was last here?
    2. What sounds are there?
    3. What is moving?
    4. What is not moving that should be?
11. Another cat approaches. Cat knows this one. It is pushy, territorial, and inclined to test whether a warning really means what it says.
12. Cat stays where she is and assesses the situation. There is no reason for a fight if the other cat has enough sense to leave things alone.
13. The other cat comes closer. Cat lets out a low growl. The very tip of her tail moves, almost imperceptibly.
14. Neither cat particularly wants a violent confrontation, but neither can simply ignore the other. They have to establish what is going to happen.
15. They stare at one another, almost frozen.
    1. Cat does not move.
    2. The other cat does not move.
    3. Cat's tail-tip twitches again.
    4. The other cat's tail moves slightly too.
    5. Each watches for the smallest change in posture or intention.
16. The other cat walks closer, testing her.
    1. Cat growls louder.
    2. The other cat responds.
    3. Cat rises higher on her legs and arches her back.
    4. Her tail is now twitching visibly.
17. The other cat presses its luck again.
    1. Cat's tail begins to flick sharply from side to side.
    2. She hisses — loud and unmistakable.
    3. The other cat still doesn't retreat.
    4. Enough is enough. Cat has made herself perfectly clear.
    5. With a roar — by Cat's standards — she launches forward, paws and claws ready.
18. That is enough. The other cat backs down and runs.
19. Cat stops rather than pursuing. The point has been made. She settles again, watching for a moment but, more importantly, listening.
20. The sun is getting lower. This does not bother her. The world is not disappearing; it is changing. Colours fade, contrasts sharpen, shadows deepen, and movement becomes more important.
21. She continues her patrol, carefully and deliberately.
22. As the evening deepens, the gardens become busier in ways the humans would barely notice. Cat hears tiny movements beneath hedges and leaves, wings settling into branches, faint scratching and rustling from things beginning their own evening business.
23. Her ears twitch and turn towards each sound. She can locate movement without moving the rest of her body at all.
24. Something small moves beneath the hedge.
25. Cat changes instantly. She lowers herself close to the ground and becomes almost completely silent. Her attention fixes on the movement. Her ears turn towards every faint scrape and rustle; each paw is placed slowly and precisely. Nothing else matters.
26. A small furry shape appears between the dead leaves. Mouse, vole — Cat neither knows nor cares. It is small, it is moving, and it is prey.
27. Cat watches, absolutely still. Only her ears move. When the little animal moves, she moves: one careful step, then another, her body low and fluid, a tiny panther in the gathering dark.
28. The prey stops. Cat stops with it.
29. For several moments neither appears to move at all. Cat listens to everything around her while remaining utterly focused on the small shape ahead.
30. It moves again. Cat advances, silent and deliberate, closing the distance.
31. Suddenly it darts away.
32. Cat explodes from stillness into speed, racing after it.
33. The little animal reaches the shed first and vanishes beneath the narrow gap at its base.
34. Cat arrives a fraction too late. She crouches beside the gap, staring into the darkness beneath the shed and listening.
35. Nothing.
36. Cat waits. It may come out again.
37. By now the last daylight has gone. The darkness does not trouble her, but standing still does. The cold begins to work through her short coat and up through her paws.
38. Then she heard a familiar call from home. One of her humans is calling her.
39. Cat looks once more beneath the shed. Whatever is hiding there can stay there.
40. There is a warm house waiting.
41. She turns for home.
42. The human is standing at the door, waiting for her.
43. Cat stands at the door for a while, considering the room inside.  It's warm nothing awful has ever happened there, but cautiousness is still important.  
44. The human makes some sound and starts closing the door, but Cat won't be rushed.
45. Eventually, after the human has closed and then opened the door again several times Cat is confident that it's safe to enter.
46. Cat walks casually in, shakes some snow off her fur and paws.
47. Cat heads straight for the warmth of the human's bedroom and starts cleaning herself.

## Chapter 2: Dog goes for his evening walk

1. Dog has the lead on and Human is taking far too long making the front door open.
2. Dog bounds around him, encouraging him to hurry up.
    1. Human has his coat on.
    2. Dog has his lead on.
    3. They are going outside together.
    4. Everything is ready except, inexplicably, the door.
3. Finally, the front door opens and Dog leaps outside, pulling Human after him.
    1. Dog is sure Human must be just as excited as he is.
4. Human pulls back on the lead and makes a noise Dog recognises: *Heel!*
    1. Dog doesn't know what *Heel* means, but he knows this game.
    2. He is supposed to walk beside Human.
    3. Sometimes he even has to sit beside him before they start.
    4. It is a rubbish game and Dog isn't very good at it.
    5. There are interesting things everywhere, and the game appears to involve ignoring all of them.
5. As soon as they are moving, Dog puts his nose towards the ground.
6. The snow has changed the familiar street.
    1. Some smells are harder to find beneath it.
    2. Others remain around the bases of trees, walls, gates and lampposts.
    3. There are still trails crossing the pavement where animals and people have passed.
    4. The street is familiar, but what has happened here since yesterday is new.
7. Dog stops at the first important lamppost.
    1. Other dogs have been here since his last walk.
    2. One of them is familiar.
    3. Another isn't.
    4. Dog investigates carefully.
8. Human reaches the end of the lead and tries to continue.
    1. Dog isn't finished.
    2. Apparently Human is.
9. Dog reluctantly follows.
10. He finds another trail further along.
    1. Something crossed the pavement earlier.
    2. Not a dog.
    3. Something wild.
    4. Dog wants to follow it.
    5. Unfortunately, Human continues towards the park.
11. Dog catches another smell beneath the snow near a wall.
    1. Food.
    2. Somewhere.
    3. He pushes his nose deeper into the snow in pursuit of it.
    4. Snow goes up his nose.
    5. Dog sneezes violently.
    6. After recovering, he tries again.
12. Human once again persuades him to continue.
13. As they walk, Dog keeps finding reasons to stop.
    1. A gate carries the smell of someone who came through earlier.
    2. A tree contains the recent history of several dogs.
    3. A patch of snow has been crossed by something unfamiliar.
    4. Human, strangely, seems determined to keep going in one particular direction.
14. Dog catches up with Human each time.
    1. However interesting the smells are, the walk includes Human.
    2. Being outside with Human is part of what makes it good.
15. They approach the wide track that they always have to stop at.
    1. It smells strange and oily.
    2. Large noisy things rush along it.
    3. Dog knows the routine here.
    4. He has to sit before they can continue.
16. Dog sits.
17. When Human decides they are allowed to go, they cross the track.
18. The park is on the other side.
19. Dog becomes increasingly excited as they approach the gate.
    1. There are dogs here.
    2. People.
    3. Old trails and new trails.
    4. But more importantly, Dog knows what usually happens next.
20. They pass through the gate.
21. Human releases the lead.
22. Dog bursts forward.
    1. He runs a short distance into the park.
    2. Then he turns sharply and looks back at Human.
    3. He waits, body alert and expectant.
    4. He knows what comes next.
23. The ball is about to be thrown.
24. Human reaches into his pocket.
25. Dog's attention fixes completely on him.
26. The ball flies.
27. Dog launches after it.
28. He charges across the park with complete commitment.
    1. Through the snow.
    2. Through wet grass.
    3. Straight through puddles rather than around them.
    4. Cold water and mud barely interrupt him.
29. Dog collects the ball from the snow and races back towards Human.
30. Human throws it again.
31. Dog races after it.
    1. This time he turns too sharply on the wet ground.
    2. His paws lose their grip.
    3. His legs go one way and the rest of Dog goes another.
    4. He tumbles into the snow and rolls over.
32. For a moment Dog is an untidy collection of paws, ears, snow and indignity.
33. Then he is upright again.
    1. Nothing hurts.
    2. Human is laughing.
    3. Dog's tail wags harder.
    4. Apparently Human enjoyed that too.
    5. The ball is still moving.
34. Dog charges after it.
    1. Falling over has already ceased to be important.
35. Dog retrieves the ball and starts back towards Human.
36. Before Human can throw it again, Dog catches a familiar scent.
37. Friends.
38. Dog drops the ball where he stands and runs to greet two dogs he knows.
    1. They rush towards one another.
    2. Sniffing, circling, tails moving, bodies bouncing.
    3. There is no need for careful introductions. They know each other.
    4. Their humans greet one another too, somewhere above the much more important dog conversation.
    5. Occasionally the hand from a friendly human gives him a scratch or a chance to smell a hand.
39. Greeting turns into play.
    1. Dog chases.
    2. Dog is chased.
    3. They shoulder and tumble into one another.
    4. Dog gets knocked sideways and rolls in the snow.
    5. He is immediately back on his feet.
    6. Nothing hurts.
    7. This is excellent.
40. The play becomes rough and exuberant.
    1. There is running.
    2. Sudden changes of direction.
    3. Bumping.
    4. Wrestling.
    5. Snow and mud go everywhere.
    6. Dog is completely unconcerned by any of it.
41. Eventually the dogs separate.
    1. Their humans have finished talking too.
    2. Dog's friends move away with their humans.
42. Dog returns to Human.
43. Human is ready to continue the ball game.
44. There is one problem.
45. The ball is gone.
46. Dog returns to the place where he thinks he dropped it.
    1. Snow.
    2. Paw prints.
    3. Trampled grass.
    4. The smells of three excited dogs mixed together.
    5. No ball.
47. Dog begins searching properly.
    1. Nose down.
    2. Back and forth.
    3. Following one scent, abandoning it, finding another.
    4. The ball is somewhere nearby.
48. Human joins the search.
49. Human points towards a patch of disturbed snow.
50. Dog knows this gesture.
    1. Human has noticed something.
    2. Dog investigates where Human is pointing.
51. There.
52. Beneath the snow and churned grass is the familiar smell of the ball.
53. Dog digs his nose in, finds it and pulls it free.
54. Human is delighted.
    1. His voice changes.
    2. His whole body says Dog has done something excellent.
    3. Dog's tail responds enthusiastically.
    4. Dog is pleased too.
    5. They found it together.
55. The ball game resumes.
56. Another human they know passes nearby.
    1. The human greets Dog.
    2. Friendly voice.
    3. Friendly hand.
    4. A scratch or rub in a particularly good place.
    5. Dog is happy to see them.
    6. The park contains a pleasing number of people who are pleased to see Dog.
57. Human moves on and Dog goes with him.
    1. Dog is free to range ahead.
    2. But he naturally loops back towards Human.
    3. They are still doing the walk together.
58. At some point Dog notices sudden movement nearby.
59. Squirrel.
60. Dog gives chase.
    1. Across the snow.
    2. Through a puddle.
    3. Around a tree.
    4. The squirrel races ahead.
61. The squirrel reaches another tree.
62. Dog reaches the tree.
63. The squirrel keeps going upwards.
64. Dog does not.
    1. Dog stares after it.
    2. He bounces around the base of the tree.
    3. He sniffs where the squirrel has been.
    4. The squirrel has escaped into a part of the world unavailable to Dog.
    5. This seems deeply unfair.
65. Human calls him back.
66. Dog gives the tree one last inspection and returns to Human.
67. The ball game continues.
68. On another run Dog spots something almost as good as the ball: a large patch of mud and meltwater.
69. Dog changes direction towards it.
70. Human calls.
71. Dog keeps going for another stride.
72. Human calls again, differently.
73. Dog slows.
    1. He looks at the mud.
    2. He looks at Human.
    3. The mud is extremely inviting.
    4. Human very clearly does not approve of the mud.
74. Dog stands between two excellent possibilities.
75. Human calls once more.
76. Dog turns away from the mud and runs back.
77. Human is pleased.
    1. Dog can hear it in his voice.
    2. Human scratches him and tells him things in the pleased voice.
    3. Dog's tail wags.
    4. Avoiding the mud was still a questionable decision.
    5. Human's pleasure makes the sacrifice worthwhile.
78. The game continues.
    1. Ball thrown.
    2. Dog runs.
    3. Dog retrieves.
    4. Dog returns.
    5. Human throws it again.
    6. Dog would be perfectly happy for this arrangement to continue indefinitely.
79. The sun gets lower.
80. Eventually Human does something disappointing.
81. He puts the ball back into his pocket.
82. Dog watches the pocket carefully.
    1. The ball is definitely still in there.
    2. Human has simply decided not to throw it any more.
    3. Humans make strange decisions.
83. Human clips the lead back onto Dog.
84. Then Human takes something else from his pocket.
85. A torch.
86. Human switches it on.
87. The beam cuts across the darkening path.
88. Dog doesn't need the torch in the same way Human does, but he recognises what it means.
89. The game is over.
90. They are going home.
91. Dog sets off beside Human.
    1. There will still be things to smell on the way.
    2. The street will still contain news.
    3. But the best part of the walk remains unchanged.
    4. Dog is outside with one of his humans.

## Chapter 3 — The Towel Escape — Dog's perspective

1. Dog can see the house ahead.
2. Dog becomes excited to be home again and starts pulling on the lead.
    1. Home contains the rest of his humans.
    2. It also contains dinner.
    3. There is no obvious reason to approach it slowly.
3. *Heel!* says Human again.
    1. Dog recognises the game.
    2. Dog still doesn't understand why Human walks so slowly when they are nearly home.
    3. Nevertheless, Dog returns reluctantly to his side.
4. Human opens the front door and calls something loudly into the house.
    1. Another human replies in a friendly voice.
    2. Dog knows that voice.
    3. Everyone is where they ought to be.
5. Dog can smell hot food.
    1. Dinner has been cooking while they were out.
    2. The smell fills the hallway.
    3. Dog can smell meat, different vegetables, and sweetness coming from somewhere.
6. Dog can smell Cat.
    1. Cat is somewhere upstairs.
    2. Dog is pleased Cat is home too.
7. Dog can smell the little humans.
    1. They have been through the hallway recently.
    2. One of them has also been eating something interesting.
8. Human closes the front door and takes Dog's lead off.
9. Human produces a towel.
    1. Dog knows this routine.
    2. Human starts rubbing Dog vigorously.
    3. Dog rather likes the rubbing.
    4. Dog doesn't like having to remain in approximately the same place while it happens.
    5. Human rubs his back and sides.
    6. This is good.
    7. Human starts cleaning his paws.
    8. This is unnecessary.
    9. Human attempts to clean around his back legs and tail.
    10. This is even more unnecessary.
10. Dog sees an opportunity and escapes.
    1. He wriggles free of the towel.
    2. Human calls after him.
    3. Dog is already running.
    4. His paws thump up the stairs.
    5. Damp fur, wet feet and assorted pieces of the park come with him.
11. Dog reaches the bedroom.
12. Cat is curled up comfortably on the bed.
13. Dog is delighted to see her.
    1. Dog likes Cat.
    2. Dog doesn't really understand Cat.
    3. But Cat is part of home and Cat is here.
14. Dog bounds straight into the room and jumps onto the bed.
    1. Wet paws land on the bedding.
    2. Water and melting snow fly from his fur.
    3. Dog pushes his nose towards Cat to say hello.
15. Cat bolts.
16. Dog is surprised.
    1. He only just got there.
    2. They haven't finished saying hello.
17. Cat leaps onto the windowsill.
18. Dog follows immediately.
    1. He jumps down from the bed.
    2. He crosses the room towards Cat.
19. Cat's tail is wagging and she hisses loudly.
20. Dog continues towards her.
    1. Cat makes this noise sometimes.
    2. Dog has never understood why.
    3. He is only trying to greet her.
    4. He rises onto his back legs.
    5. He puts his front paws up towards the windowsill.
    6. He pushes his nose towards Cat again.
21. Cat hisses again.
22. Dog hesitates.
    1. That was definitely another hiss.
    2. Cat's tail is still wagging.
    3. Dog still doesn't understand what the problem is.
23. Dog moves his head closer anyway.
24. Cat lashes out.
25. A claw catches Dog across the nose.
26. Dog yelps and jumps backwards.
    1. His nose hurts.
    2. He is shocked.
    3. He had been pleased to see Cat.
    4. He doesn't understand what just happened.
27. Dog looks at Cat.
    1. Cat is still on the windowsill.
    2. Cat's tail is wagging even more vigorously.
    3. Dog doesn't understand why Cat is so mean.
28. Humans come running up the stairs.
    1. They heard Dog's yelp.
    2. One Human examines Dog's nose.
    3. Another notices the wet bed.
    4. Human has also remembered the unfinished towel.
29. Dog looks back towards Cat.
30. As far as Dog can tell, he came home, tried to say hello, and Cat attacked him for no reason.
31. Little Human runs in.
    1. Little Human also looks at Dog's nose.
    2. Little Human gives Dog a big hug.
    3. Dog likes this considerably better than being scratched across the nose.
32. Little Human starts towards Cat.
33. Big Human says something gently to Little Human.
34. Little Human stops and backs away from Cat.
35. One Human takes Dog away.
36. The towel has not been forgotten after all.
37. Dog leaves the room still baffled by Cat.

## Chapter 4 — Cat on the Bed

1. Cat is curled comfortably on the bed.
    1. She is warm.
    2. She is dry.
    3. The cold garden is now somebody else's problem.
    4. This is considerably better.
2. She hears the front door open downstairs.
3. Human calls into the house.
4. Another Human answers.
5. Then Cat hears something else.
    1. The jingle of metal.
    2. Wet paws moving on the floor.
    3. Heavy breathing.
    4. Dog.
6. Cat remains where she is.
    1. Dog has returned from his walk.
    2. He is downstairs.
    3. Cat is upstairs.
    4. This arrangement is satisfactory.
7. There is activity in the hallway below.
    1. Human moving about.
    2. Dog moving about considerably more.
    3. The rough sound of a towel rubbing through wet fur.
    4. Human talking to Dog.
    5. Dog refusing to remain entirely where Human wants him.
8. Cat's ears turn towards the stairs.
9. Something changes below.
    1. A sudden scramble.
    2. Human calls sharply.
    3. Dog's paws hit the stairs.
10. Dog is coming upstairs.
11. Fast.
12. Cat lifts her head.
    1. The footsteps come closer.
    2. Heavy pawbeats, one after another.
    3. Dog is not slowing down.
13. Dog bursts into the bedroom.
14. He comes straight towards the bed.
    1. Large.
    2. Wet.
    3. Moving quickly.
    4. His paws hit the floor heavily.
    5. His tail is sweeping wildly behind him.
15. Dog jumps onto the bed.
16. The whole mattress moves beneath Cat.
    1. Wet paws land close to her.
    2. Water and melting snow scatter across the bedding.
    3. Dog's large head comes directly towards her.
    4. His nose is coming closer.
17. Cat does not wait to discover whether Dog intends to stop.
18. She bolts.
19. Cat crosses the room and jumps onto the windowsill.
    1. Height gives her distance.
    2. The narrow sill gives Dog less room to approach.
    3. But now she has no easy exit.
    4. She turns to face him.
20. Dog follows her.
21. Of course he follows her.
22. Cat's tail begins to twitch.
    1. She has moved away.
    2. Dog has followed.
    3. That ought to be enough information.
23. Dog crosses the room towards her.
    1. Cat's tail flicks more sharply.
    2. She hisses loudly.
    3. Dog does not take the hint.
24. Dog continues towards her.
    1. He rises onto his back legs.
    2. He puts his front paws up towards the windowsill.
    3. His body fills the space below her.
    4. His head rises towards hers.
    5. His nose comes closer.
    6. His tail is still sweeping energetically behind him.
25. Cat's tail flicks harder.
26. Dog keeps coming.
27. Cat hisses again.
28. The message is unmistakable.
29. Dog stops.
30. For a moment, Cat thinks he has finally understood.
31. Then Dog moves his head towards her again.
32. Cat's tail lashes sharply.
33. She has nowhere further to retreat without abandoning the windowsill and putting herself back on the floor beside Dog.
34. Dog's nose comes closer.
35. Cat strikes.
    1. Fast.
    2. One paw.
    3. Claws out.
    4. A single blow intended to make Dog move away.
    5. For Cat, this is not a brawl; this is a jab.
    6. If Cat wanted to do serious damage, she is quite capable of doing so. A real Cat attack is a serious thing.
36. Her claw catches Dog across the nose.
37. Dog yelps and jumps backwards.
38. Finally, there is distance between them.
39. Cat stays on the windowsill.
    1. Her body remains tense.
    2. Her tail lashes.
    3. She watches Dog carefully in case he approaches again.
40. Dog stares at her.
41. Cat stares back.
    1. She does not understand why this keeps happening.
    2. She moved away.
    3. He followed.
    4. She moved somewhere he could not easily reach.
    5. He followed.
    6. She warned him.
    7. He kept coming.
    8. She warned him again.
    9. He stopped.
    10. Then he came closer anyway.
    11. What exactly did Dog expect?
42. Humans come running up the stairs.
43. One Human goes immediately to Dog.
44. Another notices the wet bed.
45. Cat remains on the windowsill.
    1. Nobody needs to examine her.
    2. Dog did not hurt her.
    3. That was the point.
46. Little Human runs into the room.
47. Little Human examines Dog's nose and gives him a large hug.
48. Cat watches from the windowsill.
49. Dog looks back at her.
50. Little Human starts walking towards Cat.
    1. Little Human can also be unpredictable.
    2. Cat watches carefully.
    3. Big Human says something gently to Little Human.
    4. Little Human stops and backs away.
    5. Good.
51. As far as Cat can tell, Dog charged into a peaceful room, chased her from the bed, ignored her warnings and then seemed astonished when she finally made him stop.
52. One Human takes Dog away.
53. The other Big Human approaches Cat.
    1. Big Humans are predictable.
    2. This Human approaches calmly.
    3. Big Human gently picks her up.
    4. Cat accepts being held.
54. Cat rubs her face against Human.
    1. Human is warm.
    2. Human is familiar.
    3. Human is safe.
    4. Cat rubs her cheeks and face against Human with affection.
    5. Human smells a little more like Cat now.
55. Cat starts purring.
