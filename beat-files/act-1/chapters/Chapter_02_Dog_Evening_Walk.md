# Chapter 2 — Dog goes for his evening walk

## The front door

1. Dog has his lead on. Big Human is getting ready, but is taking far too long about it.
    1. Coat on. Boots on. Lead attached.
    2. Dog knows this sequence. They are going outside together.
    3. Everything is ready except, inexplicably, the door.
2. Dog moves between Big Human and the front door.
    1. He looks up at Big Human's face and wags his tail.
    2. He nudges Big Human's hand. Perhaps this will help him remember what they are doing.
    3. Big Human gives him a scratch. Dog leans into it, delighted.
    4. Then Dog hurries back to the door. The scratch was excellent, but they still need to go outside.
3. At last Big Human opens the door.
    1. Dog bounds out into the snow.
    2. His paws send wet snow and slush flying.
    3. He bounds forwards again — and reaches the end of the lead.
    4. Big Human is still at the doorway. Dog has rather got ahead of the expedition.
4. Big Human calls him back and gives the familiar *Heel!* command.
    1. Dog knows what *Heel!* means: walk beside Big Human, not wherever his nose wants to go.
    2. He comes back and takes his place.
    3. Big Human is pleased. A word in the pleased voice, perhaps another quick scratch.
    4. Dog likes getting this right. He also thinks there is an unreasonable amount of interesting world to ignore.
5. They set off together.
    1. Dog is still excited, but the lead is no longer at full stretch.
    2. He keeps half an eye on Big Human and his nose ready for whatever the street has to say.

## The snowy street has news

6. The snow has changed the street since yesterday.
    1. Familiar places look different and the scents are arranged differently.
    2. Some trails have been covered or made harder to follow.
    3. Others linger around walls, gates, trees and lampposts, or emerge from the damp ground.
    4. It is the same street, but everything that has happened here since their last walk is new.
7. Dog enjoys the snow as something to *do things with*.
    1. A soft patch gives way under his paws. He bounds through it, sending up a spray.
    2. Another patch has wet slush beneath it. It makes a wonderful splashing sound.
    3. Cold water reaches his legs, but he is moving, investigating and having an excellent time.
    4. He is not trying to get wet for its own sake; the snow simply makes the familiar walk more interesting.
8. Dog reaches the first important lamppost.
    1. Other dogs have been here since his last walk.
    2. One scent belongs to somebody familiar.
    3. Another is new.
    4. Dog investigates carefully. The information is worth the stop.
9. Big Human stops and lets him investigate for a little while.
    1. The lead is slack. Dog can take his time.
    2. Then Big Human gives the familiar cue to move on.
    3. Dog takes one last sniff and follows.
    4. He knows they are walking together. Sometimes Big Human chooses when an investigation is finished.
10. Further along, Dog finds the trail of something that crossed the pavement earlier.
    1. Not a dog.
    2. Something wild.
    3. The trail goes off in a direction Dog would very much like to explore.
    4. Big Human, however, is going towards the park.
    5. Dog glances towards him and comes along, even though the other trail had possibilities.
11. Another smell catches Dog's attention beneath the snow near a wall.
    1. Food. Somewhere.
    2. He pushes his nose deep into the snow to investigate.
    3. Snow goes up his nose.
    4. Dog sneezes violently and shakes his head.
    5. Big Human makes an amused sound.
    6. Dog looks up. Big Human is enjoying this too.
    7. The food remains a mystery; a moment later they continue.
12. The rest of the street is full of things worth knowing.
    1. A gate carries evidence that someone came through it earlier.
    2. A tree holds the recent history of several dogs.
    3. Paw prints cross a patch of snow, but the trail does not go where Dog expected.
    4. Each discovery makes him want to go closer and find out more.
13. Dog is also paying attention to Big Human.
    1. Sometimes he gets a few steps ahead, then slows so Big Human can catch up.
    2. Sometimes he looks up to check which way they are going.
    3. Big Human's familiar voice and movements tell him when to stop, when to continue and when he can investigate.
    4. A brief touch of Big Human's hand, or a scratch as Dog passes, is pleasing in its own right.
14. They continue towards the park.
    1. Dog could happily spend much longer investigating the street.
    2. But being outside with Big Human is part of what makes the walk good.
    3. He isn't just travelling to somewhere interesting. They are doing this *together*.

## The big loud fast things

15. They approach a particular place on the walk. Dog recognises it before they reach the edge.
    1. The ground has a different smell: oily, hard, with traces of strange hot and bitter things.
    2. A continuous noise rises and falls ahead.
    3. Enormous things rush past along a wide strip of hard ground.
    4. They move very fast and make far more noise than seems necessary.
16. Dog does not like these big loud fast things.
    1. They are much larger than he is.
    2. They never appear to look at him or acknowledge him.
    3. They rush past as though he isn't there.
    4. Unlike people or other dogs, they cannot be greeted or persuaded to pay attention.
17. When Dog was a puppy, this place frightened him.
    1. The noise was sudden and tremendous.
    2. The big things seemed to appear from nowhere and pass impossibly close.
    3. He did not know what they might do next.
    4. He was glad to be close to Big Human.
18. But Dog has walked here with Big Human many times since then.
    1. Big Human always stops at the edge.
    2. Big Human likes Dog to sit beside him and wait.
    3. While they wait, the big things continue along their strip of ground.
    4. They do not turn off it and chase Dog. They still never seem to notice him.
    5. Dog has learned what happens here, even though he still dislikes the noise.
19. They reach the edge. Dog stops and sits without needing to be told.
    1. He positions himself near Big Human's leg.
    2. He looks up for confirmation.
    3. Big Human gives him a quiet word in the pleased voice, perhaps a scratch behind the ears.
    4. Dog's tail moves. He knows this routine and likes getting it right.
20. Another big thing roars past.
    1. Dog watches it go.
    2. It stays on the hard strip, as the others have done.
    3. Dog remains beside Big Human. The noise is unpleasant, but it is no longer incomprehensible.
21. Big Human waits, watching carefully.
    1. Dog waits too. This is one of the places where he does not need to investigate anything more closely.
    2. Eventually there is a gap in the big things.
    3. Big Human gives the familiar signal and they cross together.
    4. Dog stays with him until they reach the other side.
22. The road is behind them.
    1. Dog does not need to know what the big loud fast things are called.
    2. He knows what to do when they are near.
    3. Ahead is the park, with grass, trees, dogs and a great deal of fascinating information.
    4. More importantly, Big Human is bringing him there.

## The park

23. Dog becomes increasingly excited as they approach the park gate.
    1. Dogs have been here. People too.
    2. There are old trails and fresh ones crossing the paths.
    3. But Dog also knows what usually happens next.
24. They pass through the gate.
    1. Dog looks towards the open ground and then back to Big Human.
    2. Big Human unclips the lead.
    3. Dog waits for the familiar release signal, barely containing himself.
25. Big Human releases him.
    1. Dog bursts forwards, suddenly free to run properly.
    2. Snow flies out behind his paws as he bounds over the ground.
    3. He makes a quick loop, then turns and looks back towards Big Human.
    4. He is not trying to leave Big Human behind. Something excellent is about to happen between them.

## The ball

26. Big Human reaches into his pocket.
    1. Dog recognises the movement instantly.
    2. The ball is in that pocket.
    3. His attention fixes on the hand. Other smells, dogs and distractions can wait.
27. Big Human brings out the ball.
    1. Dog becomes almost still, apart from his tail and the tension gathering in his body.
    2. He watches Big Human's hand, shoulder and arm.
    3. He knows the shape of a throw before the ball leaves the hand.
28. Big Human draws his arm back.
    1. Dog braces to launch.
    2. Big Human's body tells him where the throw may go.
    3. Dog's whole body is ready: legs, eyes, ears, mouth, balance.
29. The ball flies.
    1. Dog explodes into motion.
    2. His hind legs drive him forwards and his paws hammer through the snow.
    3. His ears stream back; snow and water scatter under his feet.
    4. The chase itself is glorious. The moving ball gives all that speed a purpose.
30. The ball bounces and changes direction.
    1. It lands on packed snow, skips sideways and continues across the grass.
    2. Dog follows its movement, adjusts his course and commits to a turn.
    3. He is large and fast, but he can judge a moving target and use his momentum.
31. Dog closes the last distance.
    1. One more bound.
    2. A snap of the jaws.
    3. The ball is his!
32. Dog wheels round towards Big Human.
    1. His feet dig into the wet ground as he turns.
    2. Big Human is watching and calling encouragement in the familiar excited voice.
    3. Dog races back, ball held firmly in his mouth.
    4. Returning is part of the pleasure. The game happens *with* Big Human.
33. Dog brings the ball to Big Human.
    1. Big Human reaches for it.
    2. Dog holds it for a moment in happy anticipation, then releases it at the familiar cue.
    3. Big Human praises him and takes the ball.
    4. Dog looks up, delighted and ready.
34. Big Human throws it again.
    1. There it goes! Another chase, another chance to get it.
    2. It does not matter that they have only just completed the same game.
    3. The ball is moving *now*.
35. On this throw Dog turns too sharply on a slippery patch.
    1. His paws lose purchase in the wet grass and melting snow.
    2. His legs go one way and the rest of Dog goes another.
    3. He tumbles into the snow and rolls over.
36. For a moment, Dog is an untidy collection of paws, ears and snow.
    1. He gets up and shakes himself.
    2. Nothing hurts.
    3. Big Human is laughing, making the unmistakable sounds of enjoyment.
    4. Dog's tail wags. Apparently Big Human enjoyed that part too.
37. The ball is still moving.
    1. Dog launches after it again.
    2. Falling over has already ceased to be important.
    3. He catches up with the ball, takes it and runs back for another throw.
38. The game develops its own glorious rhythm.
    1. Big Human's hand, the throw, the sudden race across the park.
    2. The ball bouncing differently each time.
    3. The catch, the turn, the run back, the pleased voice.
    4. The brief moment when Dog and Big Human are together, ready to begin again.
    5. Dog could repeat this arrangement for a very long time without finding it any less exciting.

## Friends

39. Before Big Human can throw again, Dog catches a familiar scent.
    1. Two dogs he knows are approaching with their humans.
    2. Dog has the ball in his mouth; he drops it in the snow nearby.
    3. Friends!
40. One friend approaches at once, body loose, tail moving, eager to play.
    1. Dog recognises the invitation.
    2. They circle, sniff and greet one another, then bounce apart and back together.
    3. A lowered front end and a sudden sideways movement turn the greeting into a clear invitation to chase.
41. The other friend approaches more slowly.
    1. Dog notices and does not immediately bowl into this one.
    2. They exchange information at close range: sniffing, position, small shifts of posture.
    3. Dog is comfortable giving this friend a little more time.
    4. When the second dog joins the play, Dog recognises that invitation too.
42. Dog understands his friends' behaviour.
    1. He knows when a movement invites pursuit and when to turn so another dog can chase him.
    2. When a friend pauses, Dog can pause or move away before play starts again.
    3. What looks like a great muddle of bodies is full of information Dog can read.
43. The dogs play together.
    1. Dog chases and is chased.
    2. They shoulder one another, swerve and tumble through the snow.
    3. Snow and mud spray everywhere.
    4. Dog is knocked sideways, rolls and gets up immediately, entirely ready for more.
    5. This is excellent.
44. Their humans greet one another too.
    1. Friendly voices rise above the dog conversation.
    2. One of the other humans offers Dog a hand to sniff and a particularly pleasing scratch.
    3. Dog is glad to see them, but also checks where Big Human is.
    4. Big Human is nearby. All is well.
45. Eventually the play slows and the dogs separate.
    1. Their humans have finished talking.
    2. Dog's friends move away with them.
    3. Dog returns to Big Human, pleased with his encounters and ready to resume their game.

## The missing ball

46. Big Human is ready to play again.
    1. His hand is free and he is looking at Dog.
    2. Dog looks for the ball.
47. There is a problem.
    1. The ball is not where Dog expects it to be.
    2. There is plenty of snow, trampled grass and a great many paw prints.
    3. But no ball.
48. Dog goes back towards the place where he dropped it.
    1. The three dogs have crossed the same ground repeatedly.
    2. Snow has been kicked about and pressed into the wet grass.
    3. The smells of the dogs, the ground and the ball overlap.
    4. Dog remembers roughly where it was, but that is not enough.
49. Dog begins searching properly.
    1. Nose down, he works back and forth across the area.
    2. One familiar trail takes him a few steps in the wrong direction.
    3. He abandons it and doubles back.
    4. Being able to smell many things does not mean the right one announces itself at once.
50. Big Human joins the search.
    1. He walks slowly, looking at the disturbed snow.
    2. Dog checks in with him, then returns to sniffing.
    3. They are both looking for the same thing, but Dog is searching for it differently.
51. Big Human points towards a patch of churned-up snow and grass.
    1. Dog recognises the gesture. Big Human has noticed something worth investigating.
    2. Dog goes to the indicated place and puts his nose down.
    3. There are other smells here, but underneath them is something familiar.
52. The ball!
    1. Dog follows the scent a little further through the disturbed snow.
    2. He pushes his nose into a partly buried patch of grass.
    3. There it is, wedged under the snow and flattened vegetation.
    4. He pulls the ball free.
53. Big Human is delighted.
    1. His voice becomes wonderfully pleased and excited.
    2. Dog trots over with the ball, his tail sweeping back and forth.
    3. He receives praise and an enthusiastic scratch.
    4. Dog is delighted too. They have found the ball together.
54. Dog offers the ball back.
    1. The problem has been solved.
    2. Better still, their game can begin again.

## More things worth investigating

55. The ball game resumes for a while.
    1. Every throw is a new flight, bounce, chase and return.
    2. Dog's attention keeps returning to Big Human because Big Human is the other half of the game.
56. Another human they know passes nearby.
    1. A familiar voice calls to Dog.
    2. Dog approaches for a greeting and a sniff of the offered hand.
    3. There is a scratch in a particularly good place.
    4. The park contains a pleasing number of people who are happy to see Dog.
57. Big Human moves on, and Dog goes with him.
    1. Off the lead, Dog can range ahead or make a detour.
    2. He naturally loops back and looks towards Big Human.
    3. Sometimes he comes close simply to be near him, then sets off again.
    4. They are still doing the walk together.
58. Dog notices sudden movement nearby.
    1. A small furry animal darts across the snow.
    2. Squirrel!
59. Dog gives chase.
    1. Across the snow, through a puddle and around a tree.
    2. The squirrel races ahead and reaches another tree.
    3. Dog follows at full speed.
60. The squirrel goes upwards. Dog does not.
    1. Dog reaches the base of the tree and looks up.
    2. He bounces around the trunk and searches for another way to follow.
    3. He sniffs where the squirrel has been.
    4. The trail continues into a part of the world he cannot reach.
    5. This seems deeply unfair.
61. Big Human calls him back.
    1. Dog gives the trunk one last hopeful inspection.
    2. He turns and runs back to Big Human.
    3. He is greeted with the familiar pleased voice.
    4. The squirrel can keep its tree. There are other things to do together.

## The mud decision

62. Big Human throws the ball again.
    1. Dog sets off after it, fully committed to the chase.
    2. Then he spots something almost as inviting: a broad patch of mud and meltwater.
63. Dog changes direction towards the mud.
    1. It is wet, deep, interesting and almost certain to make an excellent splash.
    2. He has already enjoyed snow, puddles and wet grass; this looks better still.
64. Big Human calls him.
    1. Dog hears the familiar recall.
    2. He slows and looks from the mud to Big Human.
    3. Big Human clearly wants him to come back.
65. Dog hesitates.
    1. The mud is extremely tempting.
    2. Big Human is waiting and calling him back to their game.
    3. Dog knows the choice he is being asked to make.
66. Dog turns away from the mud and runs back to Big Human.
    1. He has not forgotten how wonderful the mud looked.
    2. He has decided to come back anyway.
67. Big Human is enormously pleased.
    1. Dog hears the approval immediately.
    2. Big Human bends to scratch and fuss over him.
    3. Dog presses into the attention, wagging enthusiastically.
    4. Avoiding the mud may still have been a questionable decision, but this is excellent too.

## Homeward bound

68. The ball game continues for a little longer.
    1. Big Human throws, Dog races, catches and returns.
    2. There is no sign that Dog is finding the activity repetitive.
    3. He would happily keep doing this as long as Big Human wanted.
69. The light fades.
    1. Big Human eventually does something disappointing: he puts the ball into his pocket.
    2. Dog watches that pocket. The ball is definitely still there.
    3. Big Human has simply decided to stop throwing it.
    4. Humans make strange decisions, even good humans.
70. Big Human clips the lead onto Dog.
    1. Dog knows the park game is over.
    2. Big Human takes something else from his pocket: a torch.
    3. The beam cuts across the darkening path.
    4. Dog recognises this part of their evening routine. They are going home.
71. They set off together.
    1. There are still things to smell, and the ground is still pleasantly wet beneath Dog's paws.
    2. His ears and legs are damp; snow and mud cling to his coat.
    3. This has not diminished his enjoyment of the walk in the slightest.
    4. He stays near Big Human, sometimes looking up at him as they go.
72. On the way home they reach the big loud fast things again.
    1. Dog recognises the place and sits beside Big Human at the edge.
    2. The roaring things are as noisy and uninterested in him as ever.
    3. Big Human waits, gives the signal and takes him across.
    4. Dog follows the familiar routine. He knows how to manage this place with Big Human.
73. The familiar street is still full of news.
    1. A trail that wasn't there on the outward walk has appeared.
    2. Dog would like to know more about it, but they are heading home.
    3. He investigates where Big Human permits and moves on when called.
74. Their house comes into view.
    1. Dog recognises the familiar place and quickens his step.
    2. Big Human is with him, as he has been throughout the walk.
    3. Dog has run, played, searched, greeted friends, found the ball and splashed through the snow.
    4. It has been an excellent outing, and they have done it together.
75. Big Human reaches for the front door.
    1. Dog waits close beside him, damp, muddy and thoroughly pleased with himself.
    2. There is still snow melting in the fur on his legs.
    3. Big Human opens the door.
    4. They are home.
