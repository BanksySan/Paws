# Chapter 2 — Dog goes for his evening walk


1. Dog has the lead on and Human is taking far too long making the front door open.
2. Dog bounds around him, encouraging him to hurry up.
    1. Human has his coat on.
    2. Dog has his lead on.
    3. They are going outside together.
    4. Everything is ready except, inexplicably, the door.
3. Finally, the front door opens and Dog leaps outside, pulling Human after him.
    1. Dog is sure Human must be just as excited as he is.
4. Human pulls back on the lead and makes a noise Dog recognises: *Heel!*
    1. Dog doesn't know what *Heel* means, but he knows this game.
    2. He is supposed to walk beside Human.
    3. Sometimes he even has to sit beside him before they start.
    4. It is a rubbish game and Dog isn't very good at it.
    5. There are interesting things everywhere, and the game appears to involve ignoring all of them.
5. As soon as they are moving, Dog puts his nose towards the ground.
6. The snow has changed the familiar street.
    1. Some smells are harder to find beneath it.
    2. Others remain around the bases of trees, walls, gates and lampposts.
    3. There are still trails crossing the pavement where animals and people have passed.
    4. The street is familiar, but what has happened here since yesterday is new.
7. Dog stops at the first important lamppost.
    1. Other dogs have been here since his last walk.
    2. One of them is familiar.
    3. Another isn't.
    4. Dog investigates carefully.
8. Human reaches the end of the lead and tries to continue.
    1. Dog isn't finished.
    2. Apparently Human is.
9. Dog reluctantly follows.
10. He finds another trail further along.
    1. Something crossed the pavement earlier.
    2. Not a dog.
    3. Something wild.
    4. Dog wants to follow it.
    5. Unfortunately, Human continues towards the park.
11. Dog catches another smell beneath the snow near a wall.
    1. Food.
    2. Somewhere.
    3. He pushes his nose deeper into the snow in pursuit of it.
    4. Snow goes up his nose.
    5. Dog sneezes violently.
    6. After recovering, he tries again.
12. Human once again persuades him to continue.
13. As they walk, Dog keeps finding reasons to stop.
    1. A gate carries the smell of someone who came through earlier.
    2. A tree contains the recent history of several dogs.
    3. A patch of snow has been crossed by something unfamiliar.
    4. Human, strangely, seems determined to keep going in one particular direction.
14. Dog catches up with Human each time.
    1. However interesting the smells are, the walk includes Human.
    2. Being outside with Human is part of what makes it good.
15. They approach the wide track that they always have to stop at.
    1. It smells strange and oily.
    2. Large noisy things rush along it.
    3. Dog knows the routine here.
    4. He has to sit before they can continue.
16. Dog sits.
17. When Human decides they are allowed to go, they cross the track.
18. The park is on the other side.
19. Dog becomes increasingly excited as they approach the gate.
    1. There are dogs here.
    2. People.
    3. Old trails and new trails.
    4. But more importantly, Dog knows what usually happens next.
20. They pass through the gate.
21. Human releases the lead.
22. Dog bursts forward.
    1. He runs a short distance into the park.
    2. Then he turns sharply and looks back at Human.
    3. He waits, body alert and expectant.
    4. He knows what comes next.
23. The ball is about to be thrown.
24. Human reaches into his pocket.
25. Dog's attention fixes completely on him.
26. The ball flies.
27. Dog launches after it.
28. He charges across the park with complete commitment.
    1. Through the snow.
    2. Through wet grass.
    3. Straight through puddles rather than around them.
    4. Cold water and mud barely interrupt him.
29. Dog collects the ball from the snow and races back towards Human.
30. Human throws it again.
31. Dog races after it.
    1. This time he turns too sharply on the wet ground.
    2. His paws lose their grip.
    3. His legs go one way and the rest of Dog goes another.
    4. He tumbles into the snow and rolls over.
32. For a moment Dog is an untidy collection of paws, ears, snow and indignity.
33. Then he is upright again.
    1. Nothing hurts.
    2. Human is laughing.
    3. Dog's tail wags harder.
    4. Apparently Human enjoyed that too.
    5. The ball is still moving.
34. Dog charges after it.
    1. Falling over has already ceased to be important.
35. Dog retrieves the ball and starts back towards Human.
36. Before Human can throw it again, Dog catches a familiar scent.
37. Friends.
38. Dog drops the ball where he stands and runs to greet two dogs he knows.
    1. They rush towards one another.
    2. Sniffing, circling, tails moving, bodies bouncing.
    3. There is no need for careful introductions. They know each other.
    4. Their humans greet one another too, somewhere above the much more important dog conversation.
    5. Occasionally the hand from a friendly human gives him a scratch or a chance to smell a hand.
39. Greeting turns into play.
    1. Dog chases.
    2. Dog is chased.
    3. They shoulder and tumble into one another.
    4. Dog gets knocked sideways and rolls in the snow.
    5. He is immediately back on his feet.
    6. Nothing hurts.
    7. This is excellent.
40. The play becomes rough and exuberant.
    1. There is running.
    2. Sudden changes of direction.
    3. Bumping.
    4. Wrestling.
    5. Snow and mud go everywhere.
    6. Dog is completely unconcerned by any of it.
41. Eventually the dogs separate.
    1. Their humans have finished talking too.
    2. Dog's friends move away with their humans.
42. Dog returns to Human.
43. Human is ready to continue the ball game.
44. There is one problem.
45. The ball is gone.
46. Dog returns to the place where he thinks he dropped it.
    1. Snow.
    2. Paw prints.
    3. Trampled grass.
    4. The smells of three excited dogs mixed together.
    5. No ball.
47. Dog begins searching properly.
    1. Nose down.
    2. Back and forth.
    3. Following one scent, abandoning it, finding another.
    4. The ball is somewhere nearby.
48. Human joins the search.
49. Human points towards a patch of disturbed snow.
50. Dog knows this gesture.
    1. Human has noticed something.
    2. Dog investigates where Human is pointing.
51. There.
52. Beneath the snow and churned grass is the familiar smell of the ball.
53. Dog digs his nose in, finds it and pulls it free.
54. Human is delighted.
    1. His voice changes.
    2. His whole body says Dog has done something excellent.
    3. Dog's tail responds enthusiastically.
    4. Dog is pleased too.
    5. They found it together.
55. The ball game resumes.
56. Another human they know passes nearby.
    1. The human greets Dog.
    2. Friendly voice.
    3. Friendly hand.
    4. A scratch or rub in a particularly good place.
    5. Dog is happy to see them.
    6. The park contains a pleasing number of people who are pleased to see Dog.
57. Human moves on and Dog goes with him.
    1. Dog is free to range ahead.
    2. But he naturally loops back towards Human.
    3. They are still doing the walk together.
58. At some point Dog notices sudden movement nearby.
59. Squirrel.
60. Dog gives chase.
    1. Across the snow.
    2. Through a puddle.
    3. Around a tree.
    4. The squirrel races ahead.
61. The squirrel reaches another tree.
62. Dog reaches the tree.
63. The squirrel keeps going upwards.
64. Dog does not.
    1. Dog stares after it.
    2. He bounces around the base of the tree.
    3. He sniffs where the squirrel has been.
    4. The squirrel has escaped into a part of the world unavailable to Dog.
    5. This seems deeply unfair.
65. Human calls him back.
66. Dog gives the tree one last inspection and returns to Human.
67. The ball game continues.
68. On another run Dog spots something almost as good as the ball: a large patch of mud and meltwater.
69. Dog changes direction towards it.
70. Human calls.
71. Dog keeps going for another stride.
72. Human calls again, differently.
73. Dog slows.
    1. He looks at the mud.
    2. He looks at Human.
    3. The mud is extremely inviting.
    4. Human very clearly does not approve of the mud.
74. Dog stands between two excellent possibilities.
75. Human calls once more.
76. Dog turns away from the mud and runs back.
77. Human is pleased.
    1. Dog can hear it in his voice.
    2. Human scratches him and tells him things in the pleased voice.
    3. Dog's tail wags.
    4. Avoiding the mud was still a questionable decision.
    5. Human's pleasure makes the sacrifice worthwhile.
78. The game continues.
    1. Ball thrown.
    2. Dog runs.
    3. Dog retrieves.
    4. Dog returns.
    5. Human throws it again.
    6. Dog would be perfectly happy for this arrangement to continue indefinitely.
79. The sun gets lower.
80. Eventually Human does something disappointing.
81. He puts the ball back into his pocket.
82. Dog watches the pocket carefully.
    1. The ball is definitely still in there.
    2. Human has simply decided not to throw it any more.
    3. Humans make strange decisions.
83. Human clips the lead back onto Dog.
84. Then Human takes something else from his pocket.
85. A torch.
86. Human switches it on.
87. The beam cuts across the darkening path.
88. Dog doesn't need the torch in the same way Human does, but he recognises what it means.
89. The game is over.
90. They are going home.
91. Dog sets off beside Human.
    1. There will still be things to smell on the way.
    2. The street will still contain news.
    3. But the best part of the walk remains unchanged.
    4. Dog is outside with one of his humans.
