# Chapter 1 — Cat begins her evening patrol

## The back door

1. Cat is looking out of the back door.
    1. She takes in her world: the familiar garden, the fence, the neighbouring garden beyond it and the tree at the far end.
    2. Snow covers the ground unevenly. Where it has begun to melt, there is cold, wet slush.
    3. The garden is still *her* garden, but the usual paths are not quite as she left them.
    4. She tests the ground just beyond the threshold with one front paw.
    5. It sinks into wet snow. Cold water reaches between her toes. She withdraws the paw at once and shakes it.
    6. This is thoroughly objectionable. A perfectly serviceable route has been turned into something cold, wet and unreliable.
    7. The conditions do not make her want to abandon the patrol; they make her want to choose her route with greater care.
2. She needs to go outside for her patrol. First, she needs a safe and reasonably dry route.
    1. Between the door and the fence is a stretch of exposed ground, made worse by the snow and slush.
    2. She identifies possible footholds: a strip of bare paving, the edge of a flowerbed and the shortest crossing to the fence.
    3. From the fence she can see both her garden and the neighbour's garden.
    4. She knows the neighbour has a loud dog that will bark at her.
    5. The fence gives her options:
        1. If the neighbour's garden is clear, she can continue along the fence to the tree at the end of the garden.
        2. If trouble appears, she can drop back into her own garden and make a quick dash for cover.
    6. The snow matters to this calculation. She can jump and climb very well, but a poor landing on icy ground would still be a poor landing.
3. She sniffs the air and listens carefully.
    1. Her ears twist towards incidental sounds. She judges which ones might indicate danger and which belong to the ordinary evening.
    2. Something drips from a branch. Snow shifts from a roof. Neither requires her attention for long.
    3. She will commit only once she has gathered enough information.
4. There is a gentle push from behind her. One of her humans.
    1. Humans sometimes do this. They appear not to understand that investigating before moving is important.
    2. Cat adjusts her stance but continues to look and listen. The slush has not become any more appealing.
5. There is another push, gentle but more forceful.
    1. Cat has no choice now: the decision has been made for her.
    2. She crosses the open ground at speed, using the firmest patches she identified rather than stepping into the deepest slush.
    3. A splash catches one hind paw. She dislikes this intensely, but does not stop in the exposed space to deal with it.
    4. She reaches the fence and climbs in a burst of efficient movement. Her claws find purchase in the upright timber.
    5. The neighbour's dog is not there. Good.
    6. Once safely above the ground, she can shake the offending moisture from her paw.

## The patrol route

6. She moves along the narrow top of the fence as comfortably as if it were flat ground — provided she can trust the surface.
    1. Some stretches are clear timber; others hold clumps of snow or a thin, slippery film of meltwater.
    2. Cat tests an uncertain foothold before putting her full weight on it.
    3. Where the top is slick, she adjusts her footing, uses a fence post for support and moves on.
    4. This is not hesitation for its own sake. She is choosing where her weight goes, something that will matter very much when she has to move quickly.
7. She reaches the tree at the end of the garden, climbs and pauses among the branches.
    1. She can see her own garden and the neighbour's garden below.
    2. The higher branches are partly sheltered and offer a route above the worst of the wet ground.
    3. She listens before continuing. The familiar territory is still full of small things that might have changed.
8. After surveying her surroundings, she follows one of her usual patrol routes.
    1. She crosses the boundaries between neighbouring gardens, using fences, branches and other familiar elevated routes.
    2. Snow has made some of the ordinary landing places unpleasant. She takes a slightly less direct line where that keeps her paws out of the slush.
    3. She is not avoiding the outdoors: she is adapting her journey to the conditions.
    4. Eventually she reaches the roof of a lean-to, where she stops again.
9. Here she listens.
    1. What has changed since she was last here?
    2. What sounds are there?
    3. What is moving?
    4. What is not moving that should be?
    5. One of the usual small garden sounds has fallen quiet. She waits, attentive to the possibility that another animal is nearby.
    6. There is no need to move merely because she has noticed something. She can learn a great deal from where she is.

## Confrontation

10. Another cat approaches. Cat knows this one. They have encountered each other before. It is inclined to challenge
    boundaries and does not always respect a warning.
    1. **Recognition.**
        1. Cat is settled on the roof of the lean-to, surveying the surrounding gardens.
        2. She knows this position. It offers a good view and several possible routes onwards.
        3. Something catches her attention: a faint sound from the far side of the roof.
        4. Her ears turn towards it. She remains still.
        5. The other cat appears and notices Cat. It stops.
    2. **The approach.**
        1. They watch one another.
        2. Cat does not move. She has no immediate reason to.
        3. The other cat is some distance away. There is room for both to avoid a confrontation.
        4. It takes a step towards her.
        5. Cat watches carefully, her ears directed towards the intruder.
    3. **First warning.**
        1. A low growl emerges from Cat's throat.
        2. The very tip of her tail makes a small movement.
        3. The other cat pauses.
        4. For a moment, nothing happens.
        5. Then it advances again.
    4. **The challenge.**
        1. Cat's growl deepens.
        2. Her body becomes tense, her weight shifting into a position from which she can move instantly.
        3. The other cat responds with a growl of its own. Its tail moves too.
        4. Both animals watch for the smallest change in posture or intention.
        5. The distance between them is shrinking.
        6. Cat could withdraw along the roof and return the way she came. The route is still open.
        7. But the other cat is approaching her, and she has no intention of being driven away without resistance.
    5. **Final warning.**
        1. Cat's tail begins to flick sharply from side to side.
        2. Her ears turn backwards and flatten. Her fur begins to rise, making her appear larger.
        3. She bares her teeth and hisses, loud and unmistakable after the long, low growling.
        4. The other cat hesitates.
        5. Cat holds her ground.
        6. The other cat takes another step.
    6. **The clash.**
        1. Cat has given every opportunity for the encounter to end without a fight. The warnings have been ignored.
        2. For one final moment, she is absolutely still.
        3. Then she explodes forwards.
        4. The quiet, precise animal becomes a furious whirl of speed, sound, teeth and claws.
        5. Her screech is astonishingly loud for such a small creature.
        6. The two cats collide in a frantic flurry of paws, claws and flying fur.
        7. The other cat attempts to strike back, but Cat is already pressing forwards again.
        8. The entire clash lasts only a few seconds.
        9. The other cat breaks away and flees.
    7. **Restraint restored.**
        1. Cat does not pursue. She halts, facing the direction in which the intruder disappeared.
        2. Her fur remains raised. Her tail lashes once more. Her breathing is rapid.
        3. She watches and, more importantly, listens for any sign that the other cat might return.
        4. Nothing.
        5. Slowly, her body begins to relax.
        6. The confrontation is over. There is no reason to continue it.
    8. **The patrol continues.**
        1. Cat checks herself briefly, perhaps giving one foreleg a few quick licks.
        2. She returns to her position on the roof and listens.
        3. The ordinary sounds of the evening begin to reassert themselves.
        4. A small movement somewhere below catches her attention. Her ears turn towards it.
        5. The patrol continues.

## The garden at dusk

11. The sun is getting lower.
    1. This does not bother her.
    2. The world is not disappearing; it is changing.
    3. Colours fade, contrasts sharpen, shadows deepen, and movement becomes more important.
    4. Patches of snow hold the last of the light while the sheltered spaces beneath hedges grow darker.
    5. This is her time. If only the ground were not so wet.
12. She continues her patrol, carefully and deliberately.
    1. As the evening deepens, the gardens become busier in ways the humans would barely notice.
    2. Cat hears tiny movements beneath hedges and leaves, wings settling into branches, faint scratching and rustling from creatures beginning their own evening business.
    3. Melting snow makes its own little noises: a drip from a leaf, water ticking from a gutter, a soft collapse from a branch.
    4. Her ears twitch and turn towards each sound. She distinguishes these ordinary noises from something actually moving beneath cover.
    5. She can locate movement without moving the rest of her body at all.

## The hunt

13. Something small moves beneath the hedge.
    1. Cat changes instantly.
    2. She lowers herself close to the ground and becomes almost completely silent.
    3. Her attention fixes on the movement.
    4. Her ears turn towards every faint scrape and rustle.
    5. Each paw is placed slowly and precisely. Where possible, she chooses firmer, less waterlogged ground that will not squelch beneath her.
    6. A small furry shape appears between the dead leaves.
    7. Mouse, vole — Cat neither knows nor cares.
    8. It is small, it is moving, and it is prey.
    9. Cat watches, absolutely still.
    10. Only her ears move.
    11. When the little animal moves, she moves.
    12. One careful step, then another, her body low and fluid, a tiny panther in the gathering dark.
    13. The prey stops. Cat stops with it.
    14. For several moments neither appears to move at all.
    15. Cat listens, utterly focused on the small shape ahead.
    16. It moves again.
    17. Cat advances, silent and deliberate, closing the distance.
    18. Suddenly it darts away.
    19. Cat explodes from stillness into speed, racing after it.
    20. The little animal reaches the shed first and vanishes into a narrow gap.
    21. Cat arrives a fraction too late.
    22. She crouches beside the gap, staring into the darkness beneath the shed and listening.
    23. Cat is patient.
    24. She waits.
    25. Nothing.
    26. Cat waits. It may still come out again.

## The cold and the journey home

14. By now the last daylight has gone.
    1. The darkness is not a problem. There is still enough light for Cat to distinguish edges and movement, and plenty for her to hear.
15. Standing still, however, has become increasingly unpleasant.
    1. While hunting, she barely noticed the damp creeping into her paws and the lower part of her short coat.
    2. Now that she is waiting, the cold makes itself impossible to ignore.
    3. The wet ground offers little comfort, and she cannot warm herself merely by remaining motionless.
    4. She hears a familiar call from home. One of her humans is calling her.
    5. Warmth is suddenly a much more attractive prospect than this unpromising gap beneath the shed.
16. Cat looks once more beneath the shed. Whatever is hiding there can stay there.
17. There is a warm house waiting. The hunt can wait.
    1. She is not retreating from the dark or from anything she has seen.
    2. She has completed her patrol, and the cold, wet conditions have made continuing less worthwhile.
18. She turns for home.
    1. She retraces the route she chose on the outward journey, remembering which surfaces were firm and which were slippery.
    2. She crosses the exposed patch of garden quickly and reaches the back door.
    3. The door is closed, so she sits outside. Her human usually opens it again soon, and today is no different.
    4. The slush is still there. Cat is not inclined to spend any more time standing in it than necessary.

## The doorway negotiation

19. Her human opens the door. Cat stands at the threshold for a while, considering the room inside.
    1. There is warmth coming through the opening, and the surfaces indoors are wonderfully dry.
    2. But going from outside to inside is still a transition worth checking. What has changed while she was away?
20. Nothing awful has ever happened in this room, but caution is still important.
    1. Cat looks and listens before committing herself.
    2. The cold at her paws is a persuasive argument for going in promptly; it is not quite persuasive enough to skip the inspection.
21. The human makes a sound and starts closing the door, but Cat will not be rushed.
22. She allows the door to close, then stares at her human. Her human will open it again.
23. Eventually, after the human has closed and opened the door several times, Cat decides to enter.
    1. The repeated opening and closing is unhelpful, but her human does eventually provide an adequate opportunity.
    2. Cat crosses the threshold in her own time.
24. Cat walks casually in and shakes the snow and moisture from her fur and paws.
    1. She gives one particularly offensive wet paw a vigorous shake.
    2. The familiar warmth begins to replace the cold that has accompanied her all evening.
    3. She heads upstairs, towards the quietest and warmest room she knows.

## Warmth and quiet company

25. Cat reaches the human's bedroom and begins restoring herself to comfort.
    1. The bed is high, familiar and dry. It offers warmth and a good position from which to see the room.
    2. One of her humans is there, quietly occupied. Cat knows the familiar presence and does not mind sharing the room.
    3. She approaches on her own terms, perhaps brushing her head against an offered hand before settling beside the human.
    4. This is companionship too: close enough to be together, without any need for commotion.
    5. Cat begins to groom, carefully cleaning the damp from her paws and fur.
    6. At last she can stop attending to cold, wet footing and devote herself to being warm and comfortable.
    7. For now the room is quiet. Cat has every reason to expect it to stay that way.
