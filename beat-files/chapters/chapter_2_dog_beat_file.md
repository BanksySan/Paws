# Chapter 2 Beat File — Dog

## Working Purpose

Introduce Dog entirely on Dog’s own terms, during the same stretch of evening as Chapter 1.

Where Cat experiences the neighbourhood as a place of edges, height, cover, sound, movement, and small changes, Dog experiences it as a dense landscape of scent, social information, trails, encounters, and things worth approaching.

The central idea is:

> Dog assumes that getting closer will make things better.

A second guiding contrast is:

> Cat understands the world by watching what it does.  
> Dog understands the world by finding out who has been there.

---

## Chapter Function

By the end of the chapter, the reader should understand:

- Dog experiences a walk as exploration, not simply travel.
- Smell is the organising sense of Dog’s world.
- Dog gathers information by getting physically close.
- Dog is highly social and enjoys meeting familiar dogs.
- Dog is friendly towards humans and generally assumes friendliness in return.
- Dog is physically robust and largely unconcerned by cold, mud, and rain.
- Dog’s movement is energetic, direct, and expansive.
- Dog’s human is part of the adventure.
- Dog is socially competent with other dogs, even if exuberant.
- Dog does not understand Cat’s signals.
- Dog’s confidence is genuine, but it depends partly on assumptions that may later fail him.

---

## Emotional Shape

**Excitement → discovery → reunion → play → distraction → minor frustration → new discovery → contentment**

The chapter should not contain serious jeopardy.

Its job is to show Dog being completely competent at being Dog, in exactly the same way Chapter 1 showed Cat being competent at being Cat.

---

# Beats

## Beat 1 — Already Out

We do not begin at the front door.

We join Dog already on the walk.

Perhaps he is only a few houses from home, but the human already feels that progress is slow.

Dog has found something important beside a lamppost.

He sniffs.

And sniffs.

And sniffs.

The lead gently tightens.

Dog is not finished.

To the human, the walk is a route.

To Dog, the walk is whatever is interesting enough to investigate.

### Story Job

Immediately establish Dog’s basic mechanic:

**Go closer. Find out.**

This contrasts directly with Cat:

**Survey first. Enter second.**

---

## Beat 2 — The Lamppost

This is Dog’s equivalent of Cat’s lookout point.

Cat gathers information from a distance.

Dog gathers information close up.

Several dogs have passed the lamppost.

One is familiar.

One is not.

One was here recently.

Perhaps Dog recognises a regular friend.

Provisionally:

**Archie.**

Dog sniffs again.

Definitely Archie.

Archie has been here.

Recently.

Dog now has a purpose.

### Story Job

Give Dog his equivalent of Cat’s anomaly.

Cat finds:

**Something here has changed.**

Dog finds:

**Someone I know came this way.**

---

## Beat 3 — Follow the News

Dog sets off with renewed enthusiasm.

The pavement is not simply pavement.

It is covered in traces.

Dog detects:

- where another dog crossed;
- where a fox passed;
- where someone stopped;
- where food was dropped;
- where a human recently came through a gate;
- something interesting beneath a hedge;
- overlapping trails moving in different directions.

Dog veers.

Stops.

Backtracks.

Crosses the pavement.

Pulls towards a hedge.

The human keeps redirecting him.

Dog accepts this cheerfully because the human is part of the walk.

### Story Job

Show that Dog’s map of the world is based on trails, histories, and sources.

Also establish a major difference from Cat:

**Cat travels independently. Dog travels with someone.**

---

## Beat 4 — The Same Gate

Dog passes the same open gate Cat investigated in Chapter 1.

To Cat, the important fact was:

**The gate is usually shut.**

To Dog, the important fact is:

**Something interesting has been through here.**

Perhaps another cat has passed through.

Perhaps Cat herself has.

Dog pushes his nose towards the opening.

The human says:

> “Come on.”

Dog reluctantly leaves.

### Story Job

Create dramatic irony.

The reader knows why the gate matters from Cat’s chapter.

Dog can detect things about the gate that Cat could not.

Neither account is complete.

This is the book’s central idea in miniature.

---

## Beat 5 — A Friend

Dog becomes aware of another dog approaching.

His behaviour changes immediately.

Cat’s meeting with another cat produced caution.

Dog’s meeting produces delight.

**FRIEND.**

Tail wagging.

Body moving.

Perhaps the whole rear half of Dog is now involved.

The other dog reciprocates.

They approach.

Sniff.

Circle.

Greet.

Then, perhaps, play.

### Story Job

Establish that Dog naturally closes distance.

Where Cat asks:

**Do I need to get away from this?**

Dog asks:

**Can I go and say hello?**

---

## Beat 6 — Doggy Friend No. 1

The first friend is someone Dog knows well.

Provisionally:

### Archie

Archie is smaller.

Quicker.

Confident.

They already know how to play together.

Archie darts away.

Dog bounds after him.

Mud flies.

Water goes everywhere.

Dog runs with his entire body.

Cat moves in bursts:

**stillness → speed → stillness**

Dog moves more like:

**BOOM BOOM BOOM BOOM**

He does not weave carefully around the environment.

He charges through it.

### Story Job

Establish Dog’s physicality.

Dog is big, strong, energetic, and robust.

He does not need the same precision Cat needs.

---

## Beat 7 — The Puddle

This beat directly mirrors Cat.

Cat spent energy avoiding:

- wet grass;
- dripping leaves;
- mud;
- puddles.

Dog reaches a puddle.

Dog goes through it.

Perhaps he does not even consciously register the water.

Or perhaps something interesting lies on the other side and the puddle is irrelevant.

Splash.

Mud.

Cold water.

The human is much more concerned than Dog.

### Story Job

Same weather.

Different body.

Different experience.

No explanation required.

---

## Beat 8 — A Friendly Stranger

Dog encounters a human he does not know.

Perhaps:

- a neighbour;
- a jogger;
- someone waiting at a bus stop;
- someone standing at a gate.

Dog approaches with interest.

The person says hello.

Dog’s tail wags.

Perhaps they scratch him behind the ears.

Dog is delighted.

### Story Job

Establish one of Dog’s deep assumptions:

> Humans are friendly.

This is a character trait of Dog, not a statement about all dogs.

It matters later during the burglary.

When a burglar threatens or hurts Dog, the shock will not merely be:

**I have been hurt.**

It will also be:

**A human did that.**

Dog has never really expected such a thing.

---

## Beat 9 — Doggy Friend No. 2

The second dog should be different from Archie.

Perhaps this dog is older.

Dog approaches enthusiastically.

Dog offers play.

The older dog is happy to greet him but does not want to play.

Dog tries once.

Perhaps twice.

Then understands.

He settles.

### Story Job

Show that Dog is exuberant, not stupid.

Dog understands other dogs reasonably well.

This sets up an important irony:

> Dog understands dogs.  
> Cat understands cats.  
> Each assumes the other species must mean the same things.

---

## Beat 10 — The Incredible Smell

Now Dog’s nose completely takes over the story.

Something catches his attention.

Not another dog.

Something mysterious.

Perhaps beneath a hedge.

Dog follows it.

The human waits.

Dog goes deeper.

The lead tightens.

Dog changes direction.

The trail circles a tree.

Perhaps it belongs to a fox.

Perhaps it is old food.

Perhaps some animal crossed hours ago.

We do not necessarily tell the reader.

From the reader’s visual perspective:

**There is nothing there.**

But Dog follows the invisible trail with complete confidence.

### Story Job

Mirror Cat detecting prey by sound.

Cat’s response:

**Wait for it to reveal itself.**

Dog’s response:

**Follow where it went.**

---

## Beat 11 — Human Says No

Dog discovers something irresistible.

Possibly food.

Possibly something disgusting.

Dog moves towards it.

Human says:

> “No.”

Dog stops.

Or mostly stops.

Dog looks at Human.

Looks at Thing.

Human.

Thing.

Human.

Eventually Dog leaves it.

Reluctantly.

### Story Job

Establish that Dog’s freedom is constrained.

But Dog accepts that constraint because the relationship matters.

Dog does not experience the lead simply as oppression.

Human is part of Dog’s expedition and part of Dog’s safety.

---

## Beat 12 — The World Gets Darker

The light continues to fade.

This reconnects us explicitly with Cat’s chapter.

The human may become slightly more cautious.

Dog does not particularly care.

Darkness is not Dog’s great sensory advantage.

His nose has been doing most of the work all along.

Rain and damp alter the scent-world.

Things linger.

Trails cross.

The ground becomes richer with information.

Where Cat’s world becomes more strongly:

**shape + movement + sound**

Dog’s remains:

**trail + source + history**

### Story Job

Make the reader realise they have experienced the same neighbourhood twice without really experiencing the same place.

---

## Beat 13 — Heading Home

Eventually the human turns homeward.

Dog recognises the route.

He is content.

Wet.

Muddy.

Possibly tired.

The world has been thoroughly investigated.

Except there is always one more smell.

One more tree.

One more lamppost.

One more hedge.

Human says:

> “Come on.”

Dog trots after them.

Then stops at the next interesting thing.

### Story Job

End the walk with Dog still wanting more world.

Unlike Cat, who returns because the weather has made the outside less appealing, Dog is happy to continue.

---

## Beat 14 — Cat

Dog comes home.

Wet.

Muddy.

Happy.

Cat is somewhere warm.

Perhaps Dog sees Cat curled on a chair.

Dog is pleased.

**CAT!**

Dog approaches.

Cat’s tail begins to move.

Dog sees the movement.

Dog’s own tail is already wagging.

He assumes this is promising.

He gets closer.

Cat’s tail lashes harder.

Dog becomes even more enthusiastic.

Then:

**HISS.**

Dog stops.

Baffled.

Cat leaves.

Dog watches her go.

He has no idea what just happened.

### Story Job

Establish the central misunderstanding.

For Dog:

> Wagging means good.

For Cat:

> I warned you repeatedly.

Neither is being unreasonable from within their own system.

---

# Possible Closing Button

Cat disappears upstairs.

Dog looks at Human.

Human starts drying him with a towel.

Dog immediately forgets the mystery because:

**TOWEL GAME.**

Or dinner appears.

Dog is happy again.

### Story Job

Show Dog’s emotional resilience.

Cat tends to remain concerned when something does not make sense.

Dog is more willing to move on when something else interesting happens.

---

# Sensory Palette

## Smell

Smell should dominate the chapter.

Useful categories:

- familiar dogs;
- unfamiliar dogs;
- freshness;
- traces left behind;
- food;
- fox;
- damp earth;
- hedges;
- pavement;
- gates;
- humans;
- home.

Avoid translating smell into impossibly precise human sentences.

Dog should understand identity, familiarity, direction, freshness, and significance rather than receiving perfect factual reports.

---

## Movement

Dog’s movement should feel:

- direct;
- enthusiastic;
- heavy;
- bounding;
- physical;
- occasionally clumsy;
- unconcerned with minor obstacles.

This should sharply contrast with Cat’s precise movement between safe points.

---

## Touch / Weather

Dog experiences the same weather as Cat.

But:

- rain runs off;
- wet fur is tolerable;
- puddles are irrelevant;
- mud is not a problem;
- cold does not dominate the experience.

The human may care far more than Dog does.

---

## Social Information

Dog’s world is heavily populated by others.

Important presences include:

- Human;
- familiar dogs;
- unfamiliar dogs;
- strangers;
- absent animals whose scent remains.

Unlike Cat’s chapter, the neighbourhood should feel socially crowded even when relatively few bodies are visible.

---

# Character Rules Established in Chapter 2

### Dog approaches.

Getting closer is Dog’s default solution.

### Dog is social.

Other dogs and humans are potential friends.

### Dog is confident.

Dog assumes the environment is broadly safe.

### Dog is physically robust.

He can afford to be less cautious than Cat.

### Dog understands other dogs.

His social exuberance should not make him socially incompetent.

### Dog trusts Human.

Human is part guide, part companion, part constraint, and part safety.

### Dog experiences history through smell.

Someone does not need to be present for Dog to know they were there.

### Dog does not understand Cat.

Not yet.

---

# Structural Pairing With Chapter 1

| Cat | Dog |
|---|---|
| Pauses at threshold | Already out in the world |
| Surveys territory | Enters environment |
| Lookout on wall | Nose on lamppost |
| Notices changed gate | Notices who passed the gate |
| Avoids wet ground | Goes through puddle |
| Detects prey by sound | Follows a trail by smell |
| Meets another cat cautiously | Meets another dog eagerly |
| Avoids unnecessary contact | Seeks contact |
| Retreats from weather | Barely notices weather |
| Travels independently | Travels with Human |
| Returns relieved to warmth | Returns delighted and filthy |
| Finds Dog incomprehensible | Finds Cat incomprehensible |

---

# Things to Avoid

- Making Dog stupid simply because he is enthusiastic.
- Making Dog’s nose magical.
- Explaining every smell in human language.
- Making every stranger automatically friendly without making clear that this is Dog’s assumption and experience.
- Making Dog fearless in a superheroic way.
- Turning the walk into a sequence of unrelated comic incidents.
- Making Dog dislike Cat before that relationship has developed.
- Letting Dog’s friend encounters dominate the chapter.
- Making the chapter merely a catalogue of “things dogs do”.
- Introducing serious jeopardy too early.

---

# Chapter 2 Core Contrast to Carry Forward

> Cat survives by noticing before acting.

> Dog learns by approaching.

And underneath Dog’s entire chapter:

> **Dog assumes that getting closer will make things better.**
