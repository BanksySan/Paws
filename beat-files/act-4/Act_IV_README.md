# Act IV Beat File — The Intruders

## Act IV Purpose

This act tests whether Cat and Dog have genuinely learnt from experiencing the world through each other’s bodies.

They are no longer forced to see the world from the other’s perspective. They are themselves again.

The question is now:

**Can they value the other animal’s way of being?**

The jeopardy should therefore be solved not because Cat becomes more dog-like or Dog becomes more cat-like, but because:

- Cat is brilliantly, distinctively Cat.
- Dog is brilliantly, distinctively Dog.
- Each now understands what the other is doing.
- Each chooses to protect the other.

The emotional movement is:

**collaborators → allies → family**

---

# SET-UP

## Beat 1 — Christmas, and an Empty House

It is Christmas or very close to Christmas.

The house is warm, decorated and familiar. Outside there is fresh or falling snow.

The humans, including the child/children, are away from the house for the evening. The reason can be established casually earlier: visiting family, a Christmas event, carols, a party, etc.

Cat and Dog have been left together.

This is significant.

Earlier in the story, being left alone together would have been an irritation.

Now it is merely normal.

They are not necessarily friends yet.

But they have reached a working understanding.

Neither particularly needs to admit this.

---

## Beat 2 — Their Own Bodies Again

Cat is Cat.

Dog is Dog.

There should be small pleasures in this.

Cat once again has:

- balance;
- silence;
- claws;
- height;
- effortless movement.

Dog once again has:

- size;
- strength;
- his nose;
- his bark;
- the comfortable solidity of being Dog.

But after the swap, each notices these things consciously.

More importantly, each now recognises aspects of the other that previously meant nothing.

Dog can read Cat’s stillness.

Cat understands that Dog’s noises have meanings.

They have retained the knowledge even though they no longer share the bodies.

---

# THE DISTURBANCE

## Beat 3 — Cat Hears Something

Dog is relaxed or asleep.

Cat hears something.

Not a dramatic noise.

A tiny one.

Perhaps:

- a lock moving;
- a window catching;
- fabric brushing against something;
- a floorboard giving the smallest complaint.

Cat becomes still.

Listens.

Another sound.

Then silence.

That silence is important.

---

## Beat 4 — They Move Wrong

Two humans enter the house.

Cat watches from concealment.

She does not understand burglary.

She does understand movement.

These humans are different from normal humans.

Humans usually:

- thump;
- shuffle;
- bang doors;
- talk;
- turn lights on;
- announce themselves simply by existing.

These humans place their feet carefully.

They pause.

They listen.

They stay close to walls and shadows.

Cat recognises what they are doing.

**They are moving like a cat.**

They are trying not to be noticed.

That is what makes them wrong.

Possible core thought:

**Humans weren't quiet.  
These ones were.**

Or:

**They moved like cats.  
Humans should not move like cats.**

---

## Beat 5 — Cat Watches Before Acting

Cat does what Cat does.

She does not confront them.

She observes.

Where did they enter?

How many?

Where are they going?

What are they touching?

She stays above them or in shadow.

She has not yet decided what to do.

She only knows that they should not be here.

---

# DOG MISUNDERSTANDS

## Beat 6 — Dog Wakes

Dog becomes aware that there are people downstairs.

His interpretation is almost the exact opposite of Cat’s.

**People!**

Humans come into the house all the time:

- family;
- neighbours;
- visitors;
- delivery people;
- tradespeople.

Dog does not understand the rules by which humans decide which humans belong.

These people are already inside.

Therefore, presumably, everything is fine.

Cat tries to signal that it is not.

Dog recognises her alarm better than he once would have — but his own experience of humans is overwhelmingly positive.

He is not yet convinced.

---

## Beat 7 — The Ball

Dog approaches the burglars.

Friendly.

Tail wagging.

One burglar is immediately nervous because Dog is large.

Dog does not recognise the fear.

He fetches his ball.

Drops it at the burglar’s feet.

An invitation.

The burglar quietly pushes it aside.

Dog retrieves it.

Returns.

Drops it again.

The burglar pushes it farther away.

Dog retrieves it enthusiastically.

From Dog’s perspective, the human is getting closer to understanding the game.

---

## Beat 8 — Why Aren’t They Throwing It?

Dog becomes puzzled.

He makes the request clearer.

Play bow.

Tail.

Ball.

Perhaps a small encouraging bark.

The burglars freeze.

They need silence.

Dog waits.

No throw.

Another bark.

The burglars become visibly irritated.

Cat, watching, understands something Dog does not:

**They are frightened of his noise.**

This confirms her judgement.

They do not want the house to know they are there.

---

# THE LINE IS CROSSED

## Beat 9 — The Burglar Hurts Dog

Dog barks again.

One burglar loses patience.

He strikes, kicks or roughly lashes out at Dog.

It should be one sudden act rather than a prolonged beating.

Dog yelps.

The ball rolls away.

Silence.

Dog is stunned physically but, more importantly, emotionally.

This is outside his understanding of people.

He approached in friendship.

A human hurt him.

He does not immediately attack.

He is frightened and confused.

---

## Beat 10 — Cat Hears the Yelp

Cat has remained hidden.

Her normal response to dangerous humans would be:

**Stay hidden. Escape if necessary.**

Then Dog yelps.

Something changes.

The creature who has been hurt is Dog.

Not merely the noisy animal who shares her house.

Not quite her friend, perhaps.

But her collaborator.

Her companion.

One of hers.

Cat chooses to leave safety.

This is the first major emotional choice of the act.

---

# CAT ATTACKS

## Beat 11 — From the Shadows

The burglar barely has time to recover from striking Dog.

Cat explodes from darkness.

No warning.

No frontal charge.

She goes high.

Face/head/shoulders.

Claws.

Hiss.

Fury.

The burglar flails.

Before he can catch her or even properly understand what has happened, Cat is gone again.

Back into shadow.

The attack is short, precise and frightening.

Cat does not stay to fight.

She ambushes.

---

## Beat 12 — Dog Shakes Himself Out of It

Dog sees what Cat has done.

Perhaps this is what breaks his stunned uncertainty.

Cat knew.

Cat was right.

And Cat has just come out of hiding because someone hurt him.

Dog gets back to his feet.

Shakes himself.

His attitude towards the intruders changes.

Not into trained aggression.

Into alarm.

They are wrong.

They are dangerous.

They must not be here.

---

## Beat 13 — The Bark Changes

Dog barks.

But this is not:

**THROW THE BALL!**

This is the bark Cat now understands from having been Dog.

Alarm.

Danger.

Come.

Now.

Dog fills the house with it.

His size and volume suddenly matter.

The burglars’ quiet burglary is over.

---

# CHAOS IN THE HOUSE

## Beat 14 — Dog Becomes Impossible

Dog does not attack.

He runs around them.

Blocks them.

Barks.

Gets underfoot.

Changes direction suddenly.

Follows when they move.

The burglars cannot concentrate.

One is dealing with scratches from Cat.

The other is trying to avoid Dog.

Cat continues appearing and disappearing.

The house itself seems to have turned against them.

---

## Beat 15 — The Stairs

One burglar backs away from Dog.

Dog is close behind, barking.

The burglar steps awkwardly.

Perhaps his heel catches Dog or catches the edge of a stair while he is trying to watch the dog.

He stumbles down several steps.

Not a catastrophic fall.

But he lands badly.

His ankle twists.

He sits at the bottom, shocked.

Attempts to stand.

Pain.

He can stand, but only with a limp.

---

## Beat 16 — Dog at the Stairs

Dog stands above him.

Barking.

Barking.

Barking.

He does not descend upon him or bite.

But from the burglar’s perspective there is now a large dog above him making it very clear that he is no longer welcome.

The burglar gets up.

Dog follows.

The burglar limps faster.

Dog follows faster.

The situation has become untenable.

---

# THE FLIGHT

## Beat 17 — Abandon the Burglary

The burglars give up.

Whatever they were carrying becomes secondary.

Perhaps they drop things.

Perhaps a bag spills.

Perhaps they simply abandon part of the loot.

The objective is now escape.

This reinforces that Cat and Dog are not defending a particular valuable object.

They are defending the home.

---

## Beat 18 — They Run

The burglars head for the exit.

The limping burglar slows them down.

Dog pursues immediately.

This is Dog logic:

**They are running. Follow them.**

He bursts after them into the snow.

Barking.

---

# CAT THINKS DIFFERENTLY

## Beat 19 — Cat Does Not Follow Dog

Cat sees the same problem and makes a different decision.

She pauses.

Looks.

Calculates.

Where are they going?

Dog is following where they **are**.

Cat thinks about where they will **be**.

She turns away from the door.

Runs upstairs.

This can momentarily look like retreat.

Dog, if he sees her disappear, might once have thought:

**Wrong way!**

But he now knows better.

Cat has seen something.

---

## Beat 20 — The High Route

Cat races along the upstairs landing.

The window is accessible/openable/previously established.

Below:

- snow;
- garden;
- Christmas lights;
- burglars;
- Dog in pursuit.

Cat judges distance.

Height.

Movement.

This is pure Cat.

She leaps.

---

# OUTSIDE CLIMAX

## Beat 21 — Cat Comes From Above

The burglars are concentrating on Dog behind them.

They do not expect Cat from above.

Cat lands on or near one of them.

Perhaps the less injured burglar.

Claws again.

Surprise.

He staggers.

The limping burglar collides with him.

Dog arrives.

Everything becomes movement, barking and snow.

Cat and Dog have effectively attacked the problem from two directions:

**Dog pursued.  
Cat intercepted.**

---

## Beat 22 — Cat Is Thrown

One burglar, panicking, manages to grab or strike at Cat.

He throws her away.

Not a deliberate attempt to kill her.

An instinctive violent reaction to claws.

Cat flies into:

- a snow-laden bush;
- low branches;
- a drift beneath a tree;
- or some combination of these.

Branches crack.

Snow falls after her.

She disappears.

Dog does not see exactly where she lands.

There is too much happening.

---

## Beat 23 — The Burglars Escape / Are Driven Off

The neighbours may now be reacting to the barking.

Lights appear.

Doors open.

Someone shouts.

The burglars flee.

Because one is limping, they are awkward and desperate rather than slick.

Whether they are ultimately arrested can happen later and need not be central.

The important thing is:

**they are gone from the home.**

Dog has won the immediate contest.

Or thinks he has.

---

# THE FALSE ENDING

## Beat 24 — Victory

For a brief moment, everything feels finished.

Dog is panting.

Excited.

Adrenaline high.

Perhaps neighbours are appearing.

Someone recognises that there has been a break-in.

Dog may even have a flicker of satisfaction.

He did it.

They did it.

Then he looks for Cat.

---

## Beat 25 — Where Is Cat?

Cat is not there.

Dog looks towards the house.

No Cat.

Towards the garden.

No Cat.

He waits for her to appear from somewhere unexpected, as she always does.

Nothing.

Perhaps he gives a little bark.

No answer.

This is where the tone changes.

The noisy climax ends.

The quiet climax begins.

---

# THE SEARCH

## Beat 26 — Humans Look

Neighbours may be in the garden now.

They call.

They shine lights.

They look beneath things.

They search visually.

But Cat is:

- small;
- injured;
- hidden by branches;
- increasingly covered by snow.

She cannot answer.

Perhaps she is unconscious or too dazed to move.

No one can see her.

---

## Beat 27 — Dog Uses His Nose

Dog stops looking.

He sniffs.

This is something Cat once regarded as absurd.

Why stick your nose into everything when you have eyes?

Now Dog searches in the way only Dog can.

The snow complicates things.

There are many scents:

- burglars;
- neighbours;
- disturbed earth;
- footprints;
- the house;
- Dog himself.

He concentrates on Cat.

---

## Beat 28 — The Trail

Dog finds something.

Follows.

Loses it.

Backtracks.

Finds it again.

The search should not be instantaneous.

That gives tension and demonstrates skill.

Nose down.

Across snow.

Around the hedge.

Towards a tree or bush.

Then Dog stops.

There is apparently nothing there.

Only snow.

---

## Beat 29 — Digging

Dog smells Cat.

He begins digging frantically.

Snow flies.

Humans initially may not understand.

Then a branch.

Fur.

Cat.

She is alive.

But cold.

Very still.

Hurt.

---

# EMOTIONAL CLIMAX

## Beat 30 — Dog Keeps Her Warm

Dog does not leave.

He lies beside or curls himself around Cat.

His big body — something Cat has spent much of the book finding intrusive, clumsy and excessive — now shelters her.

His warmth matters.

The snow that barely troubles Dog is dangerous to Cat.

Dog understands this now.

He protects her until the humans can help.

---

## Beat 31 — The Family Returns

The humans eventually return or are summoned.

The child/children are reunited with the animals.

There is concern over Cat.

Dog stays close.

Cat may be wrapped in blankets, taken to the vet, etc., depending on how much aftermath the story needs.

The important emotional fact is that Dog does not want to leave her.

---

# ACT IV RESOLUTION

## Beat 32 — What Has Changed

At the beginning of the story:

Cat regarded Dog as:

- loud;
- clumsy;
- smelly;
- intrusive;
- foolishly trusting.

Dog regarded Cat as:

- unfriendly;
- nervous;
- evasive;
- over-cautious;
- incomprehensible.

During the burglary, every one of these traits is reinterpreted.

Cat’s caution detects the intruders.

Cat’s silence lets her attack unseen.

Cat’s climbing lets her intercept them.

Dog’s friendliness explains why he initially misjudges them — but also establishes his essential goodness.

Dog’s bark destroys the burglars’ need for secrecy.

Dog’s size creates chaos without requiring aggression.

Dog’s persistence drives them out.

Dog’s nose finds Cat.

Dog’s large warm body saves her from the cold.

Neither was wrong to be what they were.

They were wrong to assume that the other’s way of being had no value.

---

# Central Act IV Reversals

| Earlier Irritation | Act IV Meaning |
|---|---|
| Dog trusts everyone | Dog’s openness is part of his fundamentally friendly nature |
| Cat trusts almost no one | Cat detects danger before Dog can |
| Cat lurks in shadows | Concealment becomes an advantage |
| Dog is horribly loud | His barking defeats burglars who depend upon silence |
| Cat runs upwards | Height gives her a route Dog cannot use |
| Dog charges straight after things | Persistence keeps pressure on the fleeing burglars |
| Cat stops and watches | She predicts where the burglars are going |
| Dog sniffs everything | Smell finds Cat when sight cannot |
| Dog takes up too much space | His size and warmth protect injured Cat |
| Cat avoids Dog | Cat voluntarily risks herself when Dog is hurt |
| Dog pesters Cat | Dog refuses to abandon her when she needs him |

---

# Emotional Pillars

The act should ultimately rest on two choices.

### Cat’s choice

**Dog yelps. Cat comes out of hiding.**

Until this moment, Cat’s instinct has always been to preserve herself.

She chooses Dog over safety.

### Dog’s choice

**The danger is over. Dog notices Cat is missing.**

Dog could remain caught up in noise, people and excitement.

Instead his whole priority changes.

He chooses Cat over everything else.

These are the two acts that transform them from reluctant collaborators into family.

---

# Tone and Rhythm

The sequence should have three distinct textures.

### 1. Suspense

Quiet house.

Tiny sounds.

Cat watching.

Dog misunderstanding.

### 2. Chaos

Ball.

Barking.

Yelp.

Cat attack.

Stairs.

Running.

Snow.

Leap.

### 3. Silence

Cat missing.

Snow falling.

Dog searching.

Sniffing.

Digging.

Warm body around a cold one.

The emotional climax belongs to the third section, not the fight with the burglars.

The burglars provide the danger.

**Finding Cat is the real climax.**
