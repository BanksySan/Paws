# Chapter 2 — Dog

Dog had his nose against the lamppost when he found Archie.

Not all of Archie. Just enough to make his tail start moving. He pressed closer, drawing quick breaths. There was another dog here too, someone he did not know, and an older smell fading underneath. Dog worked round the post, his ears brushing the cold metal.

Yes. Archie had passed this way.

He lifted his head and looked along the pavement. No Archie. But there was pavement ahead, and the park beyond that, and Archie could be somewhere along it. Dog put his nose down again. It would be a terrible waste to walk past the right bit.

The lead tightened gently.

“Come on, then.”

Dog spread his front paws in the slush. One more sniff. He had only just found something good.

Human waited for that sniff, then stepped forward, and Dog went too. They were only a few houses from home. The snow had gone soft and grey at the pavement edges; drizzle settled over his back. He trotted through it with his nose low, looking for more of Archie.

There. His steps quickened. There again—except now another scent cut across it, and he stopped with one paw lifted.

Had he gone past?

He backed up, nudging wet grit with his nose. The familiar trace had been right here. He searched the patch again, turning his head, until he caught enough of it to step forward. His tail resumed its swing. Human was going that way too. Excellent.

At the neighbouring gate, something different pulled his head sideways. A cat's scent, close to the ground, tangled with damp wood. Dog knew this opening. The gate stood open, and he pushed his nose towards the gap. Perhaps a little nearer—

“Come on.”

He stretched his neck instead of moving his feet. There was more under there.

The lead drew him gently round. Dog looked back once, then caught up beside Human. The park was still ahead. Perhaps Archie was there after all.

Human led him through the park gate and a few steps into the clear space beyond. Dog stood while fingers worked at his collar. Click. The lead fell away.

Then Archie came towards him with his human, smaller and quicker, his body wriggling as he approached.

Dog’s whole back end joined his tail. Here he was. Here was the warm, moving Archie that belonged to the traces on the pavement, coming close enough to sniff and bump noses with. Dog circled him; Archie circled too. Dog could hardly finish the greeting for wanting to begin the next part.

He dropped his chest towards the grass. Archie bounced sideways, then darted away.

Dog launched after him.

His legs stretched, gathered, stretched again. The ground came firm beneath his paws and flew behind him. Archie turned, and Dog swung after him, ears flapping against his cheeks. They came together in a gentle shoulder bump and sprang apart. Dog’s mouth hung open. More. They could do that again.

Archie darted off, and Dog bounded straight through the shallow puddle between them. Cold water slapped his belly. Mud spattered his legs, but his paws gripped and carried him through. He came out reaching, almost alongside Archie, with the wonderful pull of running still filling his whole body.

Their humans stood chatting near the gate. Dog passed close enough to catch Human’s familiar scent, then wheeled back into the game.

When the older dog arrived with his human, Dog went over, still loose and springy from running. He knew this dog too. They sniffed, tails moving easily, and Dog bounced back into a bow.

The older dog turned his head away and stood quietly.

Dog stayed low a moment. He had been ready for the rush after him, the thump of paws. Perhaps just a little run? He straightened, then made one smaller bow.

The older dog remained still.

Dog’s tail slowed. He let his front legs straighten again and stood beside him. Then the older dog lowered his nose, and Dog lowered his own. They sniffed along the grass together. There was no chase in it, but there was someone beside him, investigating the same patch. He stayed until Human began to move on.

The lead clicked back into place. Dog followed onto the path, breathing hard and pleasantly warm beneath his wet coat.

A person he did not know offered a hand low. Dog approached to sniff it. The fingers moved behind his ear and scratched, finding a spot that made his head tilt all by itself. He leaned closer. Yes, there. His ear crumpled under the hand, and his tail swept slowly while the fingers worked.

When they went on, he walked close to Human for several steps. Then something beneath the hedge caught his nose.

Dog stopped. An animal had been here. He bent low and followed the scent towards a nearby tree, taking small, eager steps. Human stopped with him, giving him the length of the lead.

The trail curved. Dog curved with it. Then it doubled across itself, and he had to go back, breathing over damp earth and flattened grass. He found it again. This way. He worked round within reach, nose down, certain that another careful sniff might bring him closer.

But there was only grass. Earth. The trace of something that was not there.

He searched the patch once more. Nothing moved. He lifted his head reluctantly, with the unfinished trail still tugging at his attention.

Then he caught food at the base of the hedge.

Old food. Strong enough to pull him forward, though he could see nothing to put his mouth on. He leaned towards it.

“No.”

The lead shortened a little. Human called him.

Dog looked up, then back at the hedge. He wanted to go in. His weight shifted forward once, his neck stretching towards the smell. It was still there, still worth having.

Human waited.

Dog turned. One step brought him away from the hedge; the next brought him close enough for Human’s hand.

“Good boy.”

The pleased voice loosened his tail. Human rubbed his head, and Dog pushed up into the palm, staying there for an extra moment before they walked on.

Rain was pattering harder now. Water gathered at his ear tips and shook loose when he turned. Human took the familiar way home, and Dog slowed. Already? He looked along the path they were leaving, then trotted after the moving feet.

There were tree smells along this way too. Damp wood drew him towards a gate; farther on, food drifted from houses and neighbourhood dogs had left places worth examining. Human clicked on the torch. Its beam slid over the pavement while Dog followed a scent just beyond the light, then caught up beside the familiar coat.

At the next lamppost, he paused.

“Come on.”

Dog took one last sniff and went. Their house was ahead. His legs felt good, Human was beside him, and his tail swung as they approached home.
