## Direction supplied

The existing Dog viewpoint agent was asked to revise the complete chapter using `controller.md`, the Dog chapter guide, the preceding draft, and present-day character material about sensory experience, Human, emotional needs and voice. It retained the previous three directed scene packets. It was told not to adopt future revelations as current knowledge.

The brief required roughly 1,000–1,300 words of flowing close-third prose for a ten-year-old. Keep the already-out opening, slushy winter drizzle, open gate, Archie, older dog, stranger, mysterious trail, refusal and homeward walk. No Cat interaction, ball addition or new plot outcome.

Editorial direction was to connect desire to events: Archie's scent creates hope of reunion; the meeting pays off; running has bodily pleasure; an older dog's refusal brings a small disappointment followed by quiet companionship; kindness from a stranger feels good; an unfinished trail leaves curiosity; the food smell competes with Human's call; pleased voice and touch reward returning; the turn home brings brief reluctance and then contentment in being together. These were directed writing goals, not spontaneous agent discoveries.

Small compatible gestures and brief Human dialogue were permitted within the established events. No new histories or incidents were authorised. The agent wrote one revised draft without a retry.

## Controller review

The draft preserves the established outcomes and omission of the Cat encounter. It adds Human's “Good boy,” Dog looking along the path when the walk turns home, and minor gestures that fit the established actions. These are recorded editorial staging additions. No animal dialogue or access to another mind was introduced.

The controller changed “Cat” at the gate to “A cat's scent” to avoid asserting that the trace belonged to the protagonist. The unedited wording is preserved in the agent draft. No other prose edits were made in this pass.

The main improvement is a continuous line of wants and small changes in feeling, rather than sensory descriptions followed by cheerful movement. The older-dog passage retains his social competence without making declined play emotionally meaningless. The food scene makes Human's approval something Dog wants alongside the smell, rather than treating obedience as an automatic transition.

Remaining editorial judgement: whether this version feels emotionally convincing and recognisably Dog to the author. This is a prose revision using the same directed events, not an independent repeat of the scene experiment.
