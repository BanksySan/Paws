# Experiment 002 — Two evening worlds

Status: experimental, not accepted story canon.

## Purpose and sources

Introduce how Cat and Dog perceive their surroundings during separate evening outings. No interaction between them, no required conflict, no swap, and no intended lesson for either character. The reader's discovery is the point of these samples.

Use Act I's snowy evening, with patchy snow, wet slush and fading light. Source: `beat-files/act-1/Act I Beat File.md`, opening chapters; existing character bibles and `controller.md`. Individual chapter-file alternatives were not combined into this version.

Cat and Dog continued in separate agents from experiment 001. Each was explicitly told that this was an independent experiment and to disregard the bedroom events. They retained their selected personality profiles. This is a limitation: the conversations were not fresh, although neither received the other's context or the controller's purpose. Future lessons and the beat plans were withheld.

The controller supplied each character's available sensory information and resolved physical attempts. The agents chose what to attend to, interpret and attempt. No retries. Cat had four action turns; Dog had five. Stop at a natural sensory endpoint, with a maximum of six action turns each. These are opening samples, not complete patrols or walks.

## Resolved sequence

Cat listens from the back door. A scrape resolves into a visible leaf, while another rustle remains unexplained. She selects sheltered footing and climbs the fence to obtain a sightline. A small brown shape disappears beside the shed. She crouches to watch the gap rather than pursuing immediately.

Dog approaches a lamppost, recognises a playmate's scent and follows its weaker trace in the same direction as Human's walk. At the park, the ball displaces other interests. He bows, pursues the throw directly through a shallow puddle and picks up the ball. Returning it has not yet happened.

## Prose samples — controller assembly

These passages assemble resolved events and the agents' private viewpoints. They are edited samples, not verbatim agent output. The agents' own final passages are retained in their transcripts.

### Cat

Warmth behind her. Cold against her face.

Cat stood at the back door and listened.

The gutter dripped onto the paving. A blackbird stirred in the hedge. Beyond the fence came a scrape.

Her ears turned.

Again. Then a brown leaf fluttered through the gap beneath the boards and caught against a twig.

That explained something. It did not explain everything beyond the fence.

Snow covered the lawn. Slush interrupted the paving. Along the fence, however, a sheltered strip remained mostly clear. Cat stepped onto the cold stone and followed it.

Another rustle. Farther away this time, beneath the hedge.

The boards were in the way.

She sprang. Claws caught timber; her paws settled along the narrow top. Both gardens opened below her: dark strips beneath shrubs, the lean-to roof, a gap beside the shed.

Something small and brown slipped into that gap.

Cat lowered herself along the fence, ears forward.

A faint scrape came from deeper beneath the shed. Then nothing.

She stayed where she could see.

### Dog

Cold air filled Dog's nose. Earth. Food from a house. Dog.

That dog.

He angled towards the lamppost. The lead hung loose, and he put his nose close to the base.

Yes. His playmate had been here. Another dog too, unfamiliar, and something older fading underneath. The familiar smell continued along the pavement.

This way was excellent.

He followed, nose low. Human came too. Better still.

At the park, smells crossed over the trampled snow and wet grass. A squirrel flashed behind a tree. The lead came off.

Human had the ball.

Dog wheeled round and lowered his chest, hindquarters high. His feet were ready everywhere.

The ball flew.

He went straight through the puddle. Cold water splashed his belly; the ball bounced ahead, rolled over the snow and stopped against a low ridge.

There.

He closed his jaws around it and lifted it free. Cold meltwater. Familiar texture. The same lovely ball smell.

Across the grass, Human was watching.

## Assessment

The contrast emerged through action: Cat sought a position from which information could arrive safely; Dog moved towards information and then towards a physical reward shared with Human. The same cold and wet constrained Cat's route but barely delayed Dog. Neither needed to comment on the other animal.

Distinctive voice appeared in Cat's “An inadequate introduction” after the brief glimpse, and Dog's “My feet are ready everywhere” when the ball appeared. Cat's attention narrowed to a gap; Dog's shifted between scent, Human and the ball.

The controller contributed much of the contrast by deciding which sensory facts to supply. This run demonstrates that the agents can use those inputs coherently; it does not establish that they independently perceive or simulate an environment. Exact scent identities and locations came from controller resolution, not independent discovery by the model.

Limitations and points for revision:

- These are brief introductions, not complete chapter drafts. Neither agent completed its outing.
- The packets explicitly offered routes and sensory distinctions. Less directed packets would test how much the agents select for themselves.
- Small-step turns kept Cat near the doorway for two turns. For actual storytelling, longer action units may improve momentum.
- Cat's jokes about the environment can sound polished and adult. Finished prose should retain precision without making her uniformly arch.
- Dog's response describes his ears as “hanging forward”; that wording is less precise for a Springer than head orientation or ear movement.
- No shared-language violation or use of another character's private thoughts appeared. Mystery sources remained uncertain until observable evidence was supplied.

No story-source files were changed. These samples are available for author review rather than automatic inclusion in Act I.
