# Experiment 003 — Guided opening chapters

Status: experimental drafts for author review, not accepted canon.

## Aim

Introduce Cat's and Dog's different experiences through an engaging story for a reader around ten. The chapter guides supply the narrative progression; character agents supply viewpoint, motivation and voice within directed events. The animals do not interact in this experiment.

## Inputs and decisions

- `README.md`: creative intent, audience and shared rules.
- `controller.md`: updated for author-directed storytelling, chapter guidance and editorial responsibility.
- `characters/Cat.md` and `characters/Dog.md`: selected present-day personality, physical experience and voice. Future revelations and planned growth withheld.
- `beat-files/act-1/Act I Beat File.md`: parent act and opening context.
- `beat-files/act-1/chapters/chapter_1_cat_beat_file.md`: Cat's chapter progression, sensory palette and guardrails.
- `beat-files/act-1/chapters/chapter_2_dog_beat_file.md`: Dog's chapter progression, sensory palette and guardrails.

Experimental staging choices, not resolutions of source-file conflicts: use the detailed chapter guides' non-violent cat encounter, shared gate and Dog already outside; preserve Act I's winter setting as melting snow and slush with drizzle. Archie is the provisional name offered by the Dog chapter guide. Neither choice changes story-source files.

Author's scope: omit the chapter guides' concluding Cat–Dog encounter and direct hand-off. Finish Cat with indoor warmth and Dog with the end of his walk, leaving their later interaction for another experiment.

## Method

Fresh, separate Cat and Dog agents, with no inherited conversation history. Each receives a condensed profile and three directed scene packets. The controller establishes external events, required character actions and physical outcomes before requesting portrayal. The agents supply private reaction notes and close-third prose without receiving the overall theme, another mind's interior or future story plans.

Each packet covers a short scene sequence rather than individual paw movements. Maximum three scene packets per character, plus any clearly recorded revision required to satisfy the brief. The controller reviews and edits prose for continuity, age suitability and guide coverage. This tests directed viewpoint writing, not autonomous event selection.

## Controller review and edits

All six directed packets were completed without retries. The chosen sequence, including prey escaping, the other cat retreating and Dog leaving the tempting smell, came from controller direction based on the guides. Character reactions and prose came from the agents. These outcomes must not be presented as autonomous discoveries.

The controller assembled each draft from the three contributions and edited transitions, repetitions and occasional wording. Removed an unsupplied shared-stick memory from Dog's first contribution, avoided implying the cat scent belonged specifically to Cat, and removed an unsupported implication of a chest attachment for the lead. Cat's final prose retains her interest in the changed gate rather than granting knowledge of why it was left open. The gate remains open in both viewpoints; their precise relative outing times are not fixed by these drafts.

Both drafts stay within one close-third viewpoint each. Human dialogue is heard speech, not a shared animal language. The prose uses sensory information to cause actions rather than explain the theme. Cat moves through known routes and manages distance; Dog investigates traces, closes social distance and treats Human as part of the outing. The weather remains relevant to both without requiring opposite responses to everything.

These are first chapter drafts, not a final editorial pass. Cat's short, measured rhythm and Dog's quicker associations are distinct, though both still use many short standalone paragraphs. The shared gate could receive a more distinctive visible feature in a later revision if the reader needs a stronger recognition cue. That would be a recorded staging addition, not a claim that the current drafts establish more than they do.

No character bibles or beat plans were changed. The only story-system change was the requested shift in `controller.md` towards guided events, chapter coverage and responsibility for the finished reading experience.
