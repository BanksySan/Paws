## Cat response 1

- The open doorway is reassuring; the wet ground deserves careful feet.
- Familiar edges make the garden easy to read, even as colours fade.
- The open gate is a small change worth understanding.

Cat paused at the back door with warmth resting against her haunches. Outside, a drop struck the paving. Another ticked against the gutter. Beneath those sounds came the thin rustle of dead leaves.

She waited until she could place it beside the hedge. Nothing followed.

Then she stepped out.

Slush crowded the middle of the path, grey and soft. Cat kept to the sheltered strip beside the wall, setting each paw where the paving looked firm. Drizzle gathered on the tips of her ears. She flicked one, then the other. The weather could keep its water.

At the fence she gathered herself, sprang, and caught the timber with her claws. Her hind feet found their purchase. In another moment she stood along the top, balanced neatly above the wet garden.

This was better.

Her own garden lay below, its paths and corners still plain although their colours were slipping away. Beyond stood the neighbouring garden, the hedge, and the shed roof she could reach by following the fence. She knew the turn that route required. She knew where to put her feet.

Birds shuffled into their evening places. Behind a lit window, dishes clinked faintly. A gutter drop fell past her whiskers.

Cat looked towards the neighbouring gate.

There should have been a straight, solid shape across that gap.

Instead, the gate stood open.

She lowered herself a little, paws tucked securely beneath her, and watched the opening. No sudden movement. No sound to account for it.

She knew that gate closed.

After another quiet moment, Cat rose. From here she could see the gap. To understand it, she would have to get closer.

## Cat response 2

- The covered approach gives her time to examine the gate.
- Scratching takes her attention at once; the missed pounce leaves a gap to watch.
- The neighbouring cat is too close. She wants space, and holds her ground.

Cat followed the fence towards the shed in the neighbouring garden. At the roof’s edge she paused, checked the wet surface, then stepped across. Her claws found purchase. From there, a low wall ran beside the hedge towards the open gate.

She checked below before dropping onto it.

The hedge screened most of her approach. At each break in its cover she stopped, listened, and looked before crossing. Beyond the gate lay the pavement. Close by, an unfamiliar cat scent clung to the wet ground.

Cat held her nose above it briefly.

Then something scratched beneath the dead leaves.

She froze, one front paw lifted. The sound came again, small and close, near the shed. Her ears turned towards it. Slowly she set her paw down.

She left the wall and worked back beneath the hedge, placing her feet in the dark spaces between wet leaves. Ahead, a leaf trembled.

A small furry body emerged.

Cat gathered herself. It darted; she sprang.

Her forepaws closed on wet ground. The prey slipped beneath the shed before she could touch it.

She stopped beside the narrow gap. Her whiskers reached forward. Under there, the darkness stayed dark. She listened for another scratch, but none came.

When she turned, a neighbouring cat stood near the open gate, only a few paces away.

Cat became very still.

The other cat faced her across the damp garden. Behind it, the gate left a clear opening onto the pavement. Cat’s tail gave one sharp flick. Her ears angled, and a low warning rose in her throat.

For a moment neither moved. Drizzle pricked through her coat.

The other cat paused, then stepped sideways. Cat followed that movement with her eyes, keeping her feet planted. Another sideways step brought it to the opening. It retreated through the gate.

Cat watched it go. She stayed where she was, beside the shed.

## Cat response 3

- The sudden bang calls for cover; she has room to leave if needed.
- Cold water settles the matter. Home is close.
- Warmth and familiar places need no further investigation tonight.

Cat moved beneath the sheltered edge of the hedge, heading back towards the wall.

Behind her, something banged.

She was under the branches before the sound had finished, belly low, feet gathered beneath her. There was space ahead. She kept it clear.

Near the gate, a loose bin lid rocked in the wind. It rattled once, settled, and stayed still.

Cat waited a little longer. Then she eased out.

The next gust shook the hedge. Collected water spilled across her shoulders and down her back. She hunched as the cold worked through her coat.

That would do.

She climbed onto the low wall, paused at the fence, and sprang up. The timber held beneath her claws. Following its narrow top, she crossed back towards her own garden.

Light had thinned to a dim wash. The fence edge remained clear enough beneath her; the shed and hedge were broad shapes behind. Ahead, the house window glowed.

Cat dropped into her garden and took the paving beside the house. The lawn shone wetly. She gave it no opportunity.

A shallow patch of slush spread across her way. She stepped through, quick and precise, cold water pressing between her toes.

As she reached the back step, the door opened.

Warm, still air met her face. She went inside, past familiar smells, and made straight for the chair.

The cushion held a pleasant pocket of warmth. Cat sat upon it and licked one wet foreleg, working carefully down to her paw. Her shoulder required more effort. She twisted, smoothed the damp fur, then tucked herself into a curl.

Under her chest, the cushion warmed. Her paws disappeared beneath her.
