## Response 1

- Archie’s familiar scent makes the walk suddenly more promising.
- Overlapping traces deserve a second sniff.
- The gate offers an interesting interruption; Human’s cue is familiar.

Dog had his nose against the lamppost, and the lamppost had plenty to say.

Someone new. Someone whose smell was getting thin and faint. And—yes. Archie.

His tail began to swing.

Archie had been here. That was good. Archie meant running and shoulders bumping and the excellent business of both wanting the same stick. Dog pressed closer, sniffing up, down, then round the other side in case there was more.

There was more.

The lead drew gently against his chest.

Dog planted his front paws for one final sniff. A very necessary sniff. Then he went with Human, claws clicking through the slush.

They were only a few houses from home, but already the evening had improved. Drizzle settled on his ears. Cold water squeezed between his toes. Ahead lay the way towards the park, and here, along the pavement, was another piece of Archie.

He followed it eagerly.

Then stopped.

Back half a step. Nose down. Someone else crossed here, muddling everything. Dog worked over the patch until the lead tightened again, and trotted forward to catch up.

At the neighbouring gate, a different smell caught him.

Cat.

His head turned before the rest of him did. He knew this opening. The gate stood open, and he pushed his nose towards the gap, drawing in cat and damp wood from near the bottom.

“Come on.”

Human’s voice. The small, familiar pull.

But there was—

“Come on.”

Dog let himself be turned. He glanced back once, then bounded a step beside Human. They were still going somewhere together. There was plenty of walk left.

## Response 2

- Archie’s arrival turns anticipation into movement.
- The older dog’s quiet refusal is clear after one hopeful second try.
- A low hand and an ear scratch make a satisfying pause.

Through the park gate, a few steps beside Human, and then the little click.

Dog felt the lead come free. He turned, ready—and there was Archie, coming towards him with his human. Archie’s smaller body wriggled with almost as much movement as his tail.

Noses first. A quick circle, a sniff, another circle that nearly became the first circle again. Dog dropped his front end. Archie bounced sideways.

Then Archie darted.

Dog bounded after him, ears flying. Across the grass, round in a turn, back towards each other—they met with a soft bump of shoulders and sprang apart. Archie was quick. Dog stretched out after him, gaining, gaining—

Puddle.

Straight through!

Water slapped his legs and mud flew behind him. His paws held firm. He came out running, mouth open, and swung after Archie’s next turn.

Near the gate, the humans stood talking. Dog rushed past them with Archie close beside him, then wheeled round once more.

When the older dog arrived, Dog went over, tail sweeping. They sniffed their greeting, easy and loose. Dog bounced back and lowered his chest towards the grass.

The older dog turned his head away and stood quietly.

Dog waited. Perhaps a smaller invitation.

He bent his front legs again, just a little.

Still quiet.

All right, then. He straightened and put his nose down beside the older dog’s nose. There was grass to investigate together. He sniffed along for a few steps, quite content, until Human moved on.

Dog followed. Human clipped the lead back on, and they continued beside the path.

A person he did not know held a hand low. Dog stretched his nose towards it and sniffed. The hand moved to his ear and scratched.

Oh, there.

He leaned into the fingers, one ear pleasantly crumpled, his tail moving in broad, unhurried sweeps.

## Response 3

- The crossing scent needs patient work; there is nothing to find this time.
- Leaving the food smell takes one last hopeful lean.
- Human’s touch and the familiar route keep the walk companionable.

Under the hedge, something caught his nose.

Dog stopped so suddenly that Human stopped too. There. Low down. He bent over the damp earth and followed, taking small, busy steps towards the nearby tree.

The scent curved round it. He curved round it.

Then it crossed itself.

Dog went back, nose almost brushing the ground. Human waited, the lead giving him room to work. A little to the left. Back again. He found the trace among wet grass where nothing moved, followed it another step, then searched the same patch twice.

Nothing appeared.

He lifted his head—and caught something else.

Food. Old food, tucked into the smells at the base of the hedge.

Dog pulled towards it.

“No.”

The lead shortened a little. Human called him.

Dog looked at Human. Then at the hedge. He leaned forward once, stretching his neck to make quite sure that going nearer was still unavailable.

It was.

He turned and went to Human instead. A hand rubbed over his head, warm through his wet fur, and he pressed up into it before stepping on.

Rain pattered harder now. His ears hung wet against his cheeks; drops gathered at the ends and shook free when he turned. There was still plenty down here to investigate.

Human took the familiar turn for home.

Dog trotted alongside, slowing where the trees held his attention, lifting his nose near a wooden gate. Further on, food smells reached him from the houses. A neighbourhood dog had left an interesting patch by a wall.

A torch clicked on. Its pale beam moved over the pavement ahead of Human’s feet. Dog followed a scent beyond its edge, then caught up cheerfully.

At the next lamppost, he paused.

“Come on.”

One more sniff.

Then he went, tail swinging, towards their house.
