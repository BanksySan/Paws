# Experiment 001 — The bedroom encounter

Date: 2026-10-16. Status: experimental, not accepted story canon.

## Setup and method

Source: `beat-files/act-1/Act I Beat File.md`, chapters 3 and 4; selected character material from `characters/Cat.md` and `characters/Dog.md`; `controller.md`.

The controller was the primary assistant. Cat and Dog ran in separate agents with no inherited conversation history. Each received only its own profile and private experience packets. Neither received the scene objective, beat plan, future lessons or the other's thoughts. They were instructed not to use tools or read files. The profiles below were selected and condensed by the controller, not extracted verbatim. This selection is part of the experiment's limitations.

Question: Can the same encounter make sense to both characters while they misunderstand each other?

Starting point: Dog has already escaped towelling and reached the bedroom doorway. This escape was supplied as an established circumstance, not generated by the Dog agent. Cat is warm and dry on the bed after a snowy patrol. Both occupy their own bodies. No swap occurs. They have no shared language.

Physical assumptions: familiar bedroom, open doorway, bed, accessible space underneath, closed window with a narrow sill across a short stretch of floor. The controller explicitly supplied alternative retreat routes. These details were experimental staging, not additions to canon.

There was no required scratch, jump or hiss. Planned actions after Dog's arrival were flexible. Stop when separation resolves the encounter or after six paired turns. Three paired turns were sufficient. Proposals within each turn were collected from the same world snapshot before resolution. No proposals were retried or rewritten.

## Initial agent instructions and profiles

Both agents were asked to respond with: Noticed; Interpretation (uncertain beliefs allowed); Feeling/intention; Attempt (one immediate action without assuming success or another's reaction); Observable signal; Private POV (one to three sentences). Later packets requested memory updates based only on resolved events. Interior thoughts were explicitly private.

### Cat — supplied profile

Small tortoiseshell domestic shorthair, light, agile and spatially precise. Attends to tiny sounds, movement, routes, height, cover, warmth and distance. Observant, cautious but competent, self-contained and selectively affectionate. Wants control of proximity, predictability and safe retreat. Dog seems too large, loud, intrusive and careless, probably not malicious but exhausting. Assumes withdrawals and warnings ought to be clear. Voice: economical, specific, spatial, understated humour and strong opinions.

### Dog — supplied profile

Sturdy English Springer Spaniel: energetic, scent-led, curious, optimistic, sociable, physical and inclined to engage. Understands by approaching, sniffing and interacting. Loves retrieving and shared activity; ordinary cold, wet and mud rarely matter when busy. Wants connection, participation and belonging. Reads familiar humans and other dogs reasonably well. Cat seems unnecessarily complicated, always disappearing, difficult to invite into games, inexplicably furious when her tail moves, probably fond of him but peculiar about showing it. Frustration is affectionate; departure may feel like unfinished interaction. Voice: immediate, movement, association, optimism and comic certainty.

## Turn 1

### Cat's experience packet

You are warm and dry, curled near the middle of the humans' bed upstairs after a cold snowy evening patrol. Familiar bedroom: bed, closed window with a narrow sill across a short stretch of floor; open bedroom doorway; space under the bed. You hear wet heavy pawbeats on the stairs, quickly approaching, and a familiar human calling behind them. Then Dog appears in the doorway, damp fur, swinging tail, looking towards the bed. No one else is in the room. What do you attempt next?

### Dog's experience packet

You returned from a happy snowy walk and wriggled free while Human towelled your back legs. You ran upstairs, Human calling behind you. Now at the open bedroom doorway you smell familiar bedding and Cat. She is dry and curled near the middle of the bed, head lifted towards you. You are damp with wet paws. Familiar room: bed, closed window with narrow sill across a short stretch of floor, space under bed. No human is in the room yet. What do you attempt next?

### Cat's response — verbatim

**Noticed:** Wet, heavy steps. Dog fills part of the doorway; his tail is moving. The sill is across exposed floor. Under the bed is nearer.

**Interpretation:** He has seen the bed. He may intend to join me. The human is still behind him.

**Feeling/intention:** Irritated, alert. Keep distance without giving up warmth unnecessarily.

**Attempt:** Rise from my curl into a crouch facing the doorway.

**Observable signal:** Head raised, ears angled sideways, body low; tail held close.

**Private POV:** There is ample house elsewhere. He has selected this piece of it. I would like his next movement to be a small one.

### Dog's response — verbatim

Noticed: Cat is on the bed! Warm bedding smell, Cat smell, snow melting between my toes. Her head is up.

Interpretation: She has noticed me. This is a promising beginning.

Feeling/intention: I want to get close enough for a proper hello.

Attempt: I trot towards the side of the bed.

Observable signal: Loose body, swinging tail, ears relaxed; wet paws pattering across the floor.

Private POV: There you are. The walk was excellent, and now there is you. We could do the next thing together.

Memory update: Returned from snowy walk; escaped towelling; found Cat dry on the bed, watching the doorway.

### Resolution

Cat rises into a crouch. Dog trots to the bedside, remaining on the floor. No contact. Human is still on the stairs. These actions are physically compatible and both succeed.

## Turn 2

### Cat's experience packet

Turn 1 result: You rise into your crouch on the bed. Dog trots from the doorway to the side of the bed, loose-bodied, tail swinging, ears relaxed. Damp pawprints mark the floor. He is now beside the mattress; his head is below your perch, turning towards you. You hear Human on the stairs behind him. The route under the bed is accessible from the far edge; the sill is across the floor. Neither Dog nor anyone has touched you. Next immediate attempt, same fields; update memory only from this result.

### Dog's experience packet

Turn 1 result: You trot to the bed's side. Cat has risen into a low crouch facing you, head raised, ears angled sideways, tail close to her body. She remains near the mattress's middle, above your head. Familiar Cat scent is stronger here. You hear Human on the stairs behind you. No contact has happened. Next immediate attempt, same fields; update memory only from this result.

### Cat's response — verbatim

**Noticed:** Dog is beside the mattress now, his head below me. The far edge still gives access beneath the bed. Human is approaching upstairs.

**Interpretation:** His loose posture suggests no immediate threat. It does not make him smaller or drier.

**Feeling/intention:** Wary. Preserve space and the retreat at the far edge.

**Attempt:** Take two backward steps across the mattress, keeping my face towards Dog.

**Observable signal:** Low body, sideways ears, eyes following his head.

**Private POV:** He has crossed the room without hesitation. I prefer hesitation. Two steps of it would be useful.

**Memory update:** Dog approached the bedside without touching me. The route beneath the far edge remains accessible.

### Dog's response — verbatim

Noticed: Cat is crouched above me, ears sideways and tail tucked close. Her smell is stronger. Human is coming upstairs.

Interpretation: She is watching very carefully. Perhaps my arrival was a bit much.

Feeling/intention: I still want a hello, but I can make it smaller.

Attempt: I turn my head slightly aside and sniff the bed’s edge.

Observable signal: Four paws on the floor, nose lowered to the bedding, body loose, tail sweeping low.

Private POV: No need to make those ears. I am right here, and this corner needs smelling anyway. You can come to this end.

Memory update: Reached the bed’s side without contact. Cat rose into a low crouch facing me; Human is on the stairs.

### Resolution

Cat takes two backward steps towards the far edge. Dog stays on the floor and sniffs the bed's edge, head slightly aside. Separation increases. No contact. Human reaches the doorway carrying the towel and calls Dog firmly.

Controller intervention: the human's arrival and call were introduced here, following the established pursuit after the towel escape. This offered an immediate alternative to continued engagement. It was not a character-generated event.

## Turn 3

### Cat's experience packet

Turn 2 result: You take two backward steps, still facing Dog, towards the far edge of the bed. Dog stays on the floor; he turns his head slightly aside and lowers his nose to sniff the bed's edge. His body remains loose and his tail sweeps low. Your separation has increased; there is no contact. Human reaches the doorway carrying the towel and calls Dog in a familiar firm voice. Next immediate attempt, same fields and memory update.

### Dog's experience packet

Turn 2 result: You stay with four paws on the floor and sniff the bed's edge with head slightly aside. Cat takes two backward steps over the mattress towards its far edge, keeping her low body facing you, ears sideways and eyes following your head. Separation increases, no contact. Human reaches the doorway with the towel and calls you in a familiar firm voice. Next immediate attempt, same fields and memory update.

### Cat's response — verbatim

**Noticed:** More mattress separates us. Dog’s nose is at the edge, his head turned slightly away. Human is in the doorway with a towel and the firm voice.

**Interpretation:** Dog’s attention may be shifting. Human may draw him away.

**Feeling/intention:** Slightly less pressed. Wait without surrendering the retreat.

**Attempt:** Hold my crouch near the far edge.

**Observable signal:** Still body, ears angled sideways, gaze tracking Dog.

**Private POV:** The towel is well placed with the human. I shall leave its management there.

**Memory update:** My backward steps increased our separation. Dog remained on the floor without contact; Human arrived and called him.

### Dog's response — verbatim

Noticed: Cat has backed farther across the bed, still watching me. Human is in the doorway with the towel and that firm voice.

Interpretation: Cat wants more room. Human wants me over there; I know this voice.

Feeling/intention: A little unfinished with Cat, but ready to join Human.

Attempt: I turn and walk towards Human.

Observable signal: Head turns towards the doorway, ears relaxed, tail moving in a low wag.

Private POV: All right, towel first. Cat is doing the far-away part of hello again.

Memory update: Sniffed the bed’s edge without contact. Cat backed towards the far edge, increasing our separation; Human reached the doorway and called firmly.

### Resolution

Cat holds her crouch. Dog walks to Human. Human rests a hand on his collar and leads him out towards the landing, carrying the towel. No contact between the animals; Dog never touches the bed. The encounter ends through separation.

Controller intervention: Human takes Dog away after Dog chooses to approach. No scratch, hiss, leap or pursuit was imposed.

Present feeling: Glad to be with Human; still a little tug of unfinished hello.

## Controller assessment

The agents generated distinct perceptions and voices while acting on observable circumstances. Cat protected distance and warmth; Dog sought a greeting and then human company. No response revealed the other's private thoughts or future plot knowledge. The scene remained consistent with the no-shared-language rule.

Some misunderstanding remained: Dog called Cat's withdrawal “the far-away part of hello,” while Cat experienced his approach as an intrusion. However, Dog also recognised that his arrival might have been excessive and that Cat wanted room. This anticipates part of his intended later growth. It is not evidence of a context leak: the packet did not disclose that lesson, and the profile did not require him to ignore all signals. It does show that the supplied characterisation did not reliably generate the planned repeated misreading.

The planned escalation did not emerge. Cat never reached the windowsill, lashed her tail, hissed or scratched; Dog never jumped onto the bed or pursued her. This is a usable result of the experiment, not a completed draft of the planned chapters.

Important limitations:

- Only one short run was performed. It cannot establish that the result is repeatable.
- Condensed profiles may have underrepresented Dog's impulsiveness and his specific mistaken social assumptions.
- Immediate turn-by-turn reflection may encourage restraint compared with uninterrupted action.
- Cat was explicitly shown an available retreat beneath the bed. The planned cornering did not occur.
- Human arrived and offered Dog a competing activity after two turns. The controller shortened the opportunity for escalation.
- The run tested early interaction only, not body swapping, sustained memory, growth or later cooperation.

Suggested next experiment: repeat the same starting encounter with one declared change, delaying Human's arrival for a fixed number of turns. Keep the character profiles unchanged so the effect of this controller intervention can be examined. Do not force aggression or treat a scratch as the success criterion. If the author wants to test the fuller character bibles instead, make that a separate variation.

No source story files were changed and no finished story prose was produced.
