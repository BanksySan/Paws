# Vision

## 1. Audience and setting

1. A children's story aimed at readers around ten years old, written in British English (`en-GB`).
2. The story takes place close to Christmas.
3. Cat and Dog live together in an ordinary family home with their human owners and the family's children.

## 2. Premise

4. Cat and Dog do not get along particularly well, largely because they misunderstand one another.
   - Cat experiences Dog as loud, aggressive and clumsy. His size, enthusiasm and physical approach can seem threatening.
   - Dog regards Cat as unnecessarily nervous, distant and unfriendly. Her reluctance to engage is incomprehensible to him.
5. Both animals interpret the other's behaviour through their own experience of the world. Neither fully understands why the other behaves as they do.
6. Cat and Dog unexpectedly swap bodies. Each must learn to function with the other's senses, physical abilities and vulnerabilities.
7. Through this experience, and ultimately through facing genuine danger together, they discover that the other's seemingly unreasonable behaviour often makes perfect sense from the other's perspective.
8. When they return to their own bodies, neither has fundamentally changed who they are, but both have changed how they understand and treat the other.

## 3. Purpose

The central theme is **understanding another's point of view**.

The story should show that:

- Two individuals can experience the same situation very differently.
- Behaviour that appears foolish, unreasonable or unfriendly may have perfectly sensible reasons.
- Understanding another's perspective does not require agreeing with them or becoming like them.
- Different abilities and ways of approaching problems can complement one another.
- Genuine understanding should lead to consideration, trust and cooperation.

## 4. Reader experience

The story should be:

- **Capable of humour:** Funny moments may arise naturally from events, misunderstandings and the animals' differing perspectives, but the story is not primarily a comedy.
- **Adventurous:** The characters experience discovery, challenges and genuine jeopardy.
- **Warm:** Despite their differences, the growing relationship between Cat and Dog should be emotionally satisfying.
- **Accessible:** The language, events and ideas should be engaging and understandable to a ten-year-old without being condescending.
- **Thought-provoking:** The reader should come to understand the central theme through the characters' experiences, rather than through explicit moral instruction.

## 5. Guiding creative principles

1. **Neither character is wrong simply for being different.** Both have valid reasons for behaving as they do.
2. **Cat and Dog are animals, not humans in animal costumes.** Their personalities and decisions should arise from their bodies, senses, instincts and experiences rather than imposed human stereotypes.
3. **Neither character is morally superior.** Each has strengths, vulnerabilities and misunderstandings.
4. **The reader should discover things alongside, and sometimes before, the characters.** Seeing both viewpoints should allow the reader to recognise misunderstandings the animals themselves cannot yet see.
5. **The lesson must be experienced, not preached.** Understanding emerges through events, discoveries, mistakes and consequences.
6. **Their differences remain important.** The resolution is not that Cat becomes more dog-like or Dog more cat-like, but that each learns to appreciate and accommodate the other.
7. **They are not opposites**. Cat is not the opposite of Dog, nor Dog of Cat. They are distinct individuals whose personalities, motivations and behaviours may overlap or diverge to varying degrees. Their differences should arise naturally from who they are and how they experience the world, not from an artificial need to contrast them.
8. **Humans are secondary characters** The humans are important parts of Cat and Dog's lives, but the story is experienced entirely through the animals' perspectives. Human behaviour, emotions and intentions are understood through what Cat and Dog can observe, recognise, remember and infer. Their interpretations may be accurate, incomplete or mistaken.
9. **The world views are their individual worlds** Cat and Dog experience and interpret the world through their own senses, capabilities, needs and accumulated experiences. They live among human objects, devices and behaviours, and these are familiar parts of their everyday lives. However, they do not necessarily understand their purpose or significance in human terms. Instead, each has developed their own understanding of what things are, what they do, and how they matter.   For example, consider Dog's lead:
   * To a human, it is a means of keeping Dog safe and under control, and part of responsible dog ownership.
   * To Dog, it is a wonderfully exciting signal that he is going for a walk.
   * To Cat, it is something that smells of Dog, is of little interest in itself, but may signal that Dog is about to leave the house — potentially a welcome development.
