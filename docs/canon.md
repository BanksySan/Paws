# Canon

## Purpose and authority

This document records the **established facts and rules** of Cat and Dog's fictional world. It is a reference for authors and agents: a new scene may add detail, but must not quietly contradict what is established here.

This is **not** a creative brief, a character bible, a chapter plan or a record of every event. `docs/vision.md` defines the book's creative intentions; `characters/Cat.md` and `characters/Dog.md` describe the individuals; act and chapter files determine what happens. A character's belief about the world is not automatically an objective fact about it.

The examples below illustrate rules. **They do not require particular scenes to occur.** Anything not specified remains unspecified, rather than being an invitation to invent a permanent rule.

## 1. The world and its inhabitants

1. The story takes place close to Christmas, in a recognisably ordinary domestic world. Apart from the body swap, the world operates normally.
2. Cat and Dog live in the same family home with their humans, including children. Neither the family's precise composition nor the house's layout is fixed by this document.
3. **Cat** is a female, small-ish tortoiseshell domestic shorthair (moggie).
4. **Dog** is a male English Springer Spaniel. He is substantially larger, heavier and stronger than Cat.
5. Cat and Dog are distinct individuals with their own histories, relationships, preferences and temperaments. Species and breed affect the possibilities of their lives; they do not determine their personalities.
6. The humans' objects, devices and routines have their ordinary physical properties and functions. Cat and Dog are familiar with many of them through experience, though their understanding need not match a human's.

## 2. Bodies, senses and physical limitations

1. The characteristics of a body remain with that **body**, whoever occupies it: its size, strength, weight, balance, movement, senses, physical limitations and vulnerabilities.
2. Dog's body provides the olfactory capabilities of a Springer Spaniel. Smell can disclose information about individuals, places and traces of earlier activity; it is not magical or infallible.
3. Cat's body provides feline agility, balance, rapid reactions, precise movement, whisker-based sensation, acute hearing and effective vision in low light. It cannot see in total darkness.
4. Both bodies use multiple senses. Dog can see and hear; Cat can smell. Differences in their dominant sources of information must not become absolute abilities or disabilities.
5. Size and capability change the practical meaning of a situation. An approach, collision, jump, gap or threat can be experienced differently by a robust Springer and a small cat.
6. The physical world imposes consequences. Neither body is invulnerable, and neither acquires supernatural abilities as an incidental benefit of the swap.

## 3. What the body swap transfers

Cat and Dog exchange bodies, **not identities**. Keep the identity and the occupied body separate at every point in the story.

| Stays with the individual | Belongs to the occupied body |
| --- | --- |
| Identity and personality | Size, strength, build and physical vulnerabilities |
| Memories, beliefs and accumulated knowledge | Senses and the sensations they produce |
| Existing motivations, preferences, attachments and fears | Reflexes, reaction times and physical coordination |
| Learned understanding, habits and acquired skills | Innate physical and behavioural predispositions |

The consequences of this division are:

1. **Cat remains Cat inside Dog's body; Dog remains Dog inside Cat's body.** Neither acquires the other's personality, memories, opinions or learned understanding.
2. Both nevertheless experience the bodily reflexes, physiological responses and innate predispositions of their new bodies. A movement or impulse may occur before the individual has fully understood it.
3. A body's responses can make an unfamiliar activity feel natural, exciting, satisfying, uncomfortable or frightening. That experience can change what the individual chooses to do next; it does not mean the other's personality has taken over.
4. **Innate response is not acquired expertise.** Dog-in-Cat may have feline reaction time, sensitive whiskers and a body that can crouch and spring without having Cat's practised judgement as a hunter. Cat-in-Dog may feel the pleasure of powerful running or respond to a play invitation without inheriting Dog's particular friendships or social experience.
5. Skills and expectations acquired in the original body may be difficult or impossible to carry out in the unfamiliar one. Both must discover how their new capabilities work and learn to use them effectively.
6. Neither becomes instantly competent or immediately understands what the other has always experienced. Discoveries, mistakes and changing opinions must follow from lived experience.

## 4. Knowledge, perception and interpretation

1. Cat and Dog can remember, anticipate, recognise patterns, draw conclusions, form opinions and learn. They can also overlook evidence, make mistaken assumptions and change their minds.
2. Each understands the world through their own senses, physical circumstances, needs and accumulated experience. They may recognise the same thing yet attach different meanings to it.
3. Familiarity with a human object is **not** the same as understanding its human purpose. For example, Dog may know that a lead predicts a walk, while Cat may chiefly recognise its smell and its association with Dog leaving the house. Neither requires a concept of responsible pet ownership.
4. Knowledge does not arrive merely because a character occupies a different body. The body supplies new experiences and information, not the previous occupant's explanations of them.
5. Each character knows only what they have experienced, remembered, observed, learnt or reasonably inferred. Neither has access to the other's private thoughts or to facts known only to the narrator or author.
6. Understanding may improve after the swap without becoming complete or faultless. Neither animal must consciously articulate every lesson they learn.

## 5. Communication and misunderstanding

1. Cat and Dog **do communicate**, but they have no shared spoken language and cannot hold human-style conversations. They do not exchange thoughts, use telepathy or give one another precise verbal instructions.
2. Communication is conveyed through observable behaviour: posture, ears, tails, gaze, body tension, movement, approach and retreat, vocalisations, touch and actions within their surroundings.
3. Each has learnt some of the other's signals through living together. That knowledge is partial: a signal can be missed, misread or understood only after consequences make its meaning clear.
4. **Imperfect communication is a source of conflict from the beginning.** Dog may wag his tail in excitement and invitation, while Cat may flick or lash hers to warn him away. Dog can interpret the moving tail through his own experience and approach when Cat wants him to stop. Her escalating warning may then seem to him to come from nowhere.
5. No single gesture has a fixed translation independent of context. A wag is not always happiness, nor is every movement of Cat's tail a warning. The whole animal and situation matter.
6. They can learn through experience: Dog may already recognise that a hiss, raised paw or claws mean he has come too close, even while misunderstanding earlier and subtler signals.
7. After the swap, each can become better at recognising the other's signals **and at choosing signals the other is likely to recognise**. For example, Cat-in-Dog might direct Dog-in-Cat's attention with a movement of her new nose because she has learnt that Dog notices such cues.
8. Such communication remains behavioural, contextual and imperfect. Cooperation must not depend upon silently converting gestures into exact sentences or instructions.

**Narrative convention:** A viewpoint character's *private* thoughts may be rendered in ordinary, clear English for the reader. This does not mean the animals literally think or speak English, or that another character can hear the narrated thoughts. Detailed prose style belongs in the writing guidance, not in these world rules.

## 6. The unexplained Christmas miracle

1. The exchange of bodies is an extraordinary, unexplained event close to Christmas. It may be understood as a quiet **Christmas miracle**, not as the beginning of an elaborate magical system.
2. No fairy godmother, spell, magical intermediary or discoverable mechanism is established as its cause. The story does not need to explain why the swap occurs.
3. A subtle unusual sight — perhaps a light or a strange star — *may* appear around the event, but none is required, and no such detail should establish a causal explanation by default.
4. The body swap does not itself grant speech, telepathy, knowledge, new personalities or other magical powers. Its extraordinary element is the exchange; the animals' subsequent experiences obey the physical and behavioural rules above.
5. Cat and Dog eventually return to their own bodies. The cause and precise circumstances of the return need not be explained here.
6. They retain the memories and understanding gained while swapped. Returning restores their bodies, **not their former ignorance**.

## 7. The relationship and continuity

1. At the beginning, Cat and Dog do not get along particularly well. Repeated misunderstandings contribute to their friction; neither is objectively the villain or the defective version of the other.
2. The animals are **not opposites**. Their interests, motivations, responses and preferences can overlap as readily as they can differ. They may react alike for different reasons or react differently to the same situation.
3. Each has a history with the other before the swap. They need not be strangers to one another's behaviour simply because some of its meaning is misunderstood.
4. The swap creates opportunities for new understanding; it does not automatically produce friendship, forgiveness or flawless cooperation.
5. Experiences can change beliefs and behaviour while the underlying individuals remain themselves. Later actions must remain consistent with what each has actually had the opportunity to learn.

## Using and extending this canon

- **Objective fact is not character opinion.** “Dog is larger than Cat” is a world fact; “Dog is clumsy” may be Cat's judgement.
- **An example is not a mandatory plot beat.** A dog greeting, a mouse hunt, a nose-pointing gesture or a strange light must not be treated as an event that has already happened merely because it appears here.
- **Unspecified is not established.** Do not infer the family's names, exact house layout, precise weather, a magical cause or the timing of the return relative to the main danger from this document.
- **Canon changes deliberately.** When a new enduring fact is agreed, update the appropriate source. Scene-specific events and unresolved alternatives remain in their act or chapter files unless they genuinely establish a world-wide rule or persistent fact.
